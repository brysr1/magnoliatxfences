<h2>What it looks like</h2>
<p>Ornamental fencing &mdash; often called wrought iron, though today it's usually aluminum or steel &mdash; uses slim vertical pickets between horizontal rails. Options include flat or spear tops, finials, decorative rings, and arched gates.</p>
<p>Residential ornamental fences are typically four to five feet tall, with taller panels for entries and estate frontage. Because you can see straight through them, they're the go-to fence where a view of a lake, golf course, or landscaped yard matters.</p>
</div>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Privacy level:</strong> Low privacy, full view</li>
<li><strong>Typical height:</strong> 4&ndash;5 ft typical; taller at entries</li>
</ul>
</section>

<section class="section76-alt full-bleed76">
<div class="inner76">
<h2>When an ornamental fence makes sense</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Lakefront and golf-course lots.</strong> The view is why you bought the lot, and many lake and golf communities call for open fencing.</li>
<li><strong>Around a pool.</strong> You can see the pool from the house, and pool-code styles meet barrier rules. See <a href="/fence-styles/pool-enclosure/">pool enclosures</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Front yards and entries.</strong> A see-through fence gives the house a finished edge without hiding it.</li>
<li><strong>Acreage entrances.</strong> Ornamental panels and a matching drive gate make a strong first impression.</li>
<li><strong>Not for privacy.</strong> If you need to block a view, see <a href="/fence-styles/privacy/">privacy fences</a>.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>

<h2>Acreage or subdivision lot?</h2>
<div class="cards76">
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On acreage</h3><p>On acreage, ornamental metal is usually used where it makes the biggest impression: entry and drive gates, the front of the property, and the yard around the house.</p></div></div>
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On subdivision lots</h3><p>On golf-course and lakefront lots, ornamental metal is often the style guidelines call for, and it works well for front yards and pool areas on any lot. If your property has HOA or deed restrictions, check with your board on height and finish.</p></div></div>
</div>

<div class="prose76">
<h2>Local rules</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>City of Magnolia.</strong> Inside city limits, a fence needs a city permit: $65 plus a $50 administrative fee on the November 2024 schedule.</li>
<li><strong>City of Montgomery.</strong> Fences are on the city's list of projects that need a permit.</li>
<li><strong>Unincorporated Montgomery County.</strong> A typical residential fence usually doesn't need a county permit, but HOA or POA approval may still apply.</li>
<li><strong>Lake and golf communities.</strong> Architectural committees in Bentwater, Walden, April Sound, and Grand Harbor review fence and pool barrier plans.</li>
<li><strong>Lake Conroe.</strong> On west-shore lots, the back of the lot can run into land the San Jacinto River Authority controls. We keep the fence off it.</li>
</ul>
<p>See <a href="/service-areas/magnolia-tx/">fencing in Magnolia</a> and our <a href="/faq/fence-permits-magnolia-montgomery/">permit guide</a>.</p>

<section class="section76-alt full-bleed76">
<div class="inner76">
<h2>What affects the cost</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Material.</strong> On our <a href="/pricing-guide/">pricing guide</a>, aluminum and steel both typically run $40&ndash;$100 per linear foot installed.</li>
<li><strong>Design.</strong> Two rails or three, picket style, and top style all change the price.</li>
<li><strong>Height.</strong> Taller panels and posts cost more.</li>
<li><strong>Gates.</strong> Walk gates, drive gates, and openers are priced on their own.</li>
<li><strong>Ground.</strong> Slopes toward the lake need racked or stepped panels.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2>Ornamental fence questions</h2>
<div class="faq76">
<details><summary>Aluminum or steel?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Both give the same look. Aluminum doesn't rust, which makes it the usual pick near the water and around pools. Steel is heavier and stiffer, so it's the stronger choice for drive gates and frontage. Many owners use both. See <a href="/resources/aluminum-vs-steel-fence/">aluminum vs. steel</a>.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Will an ornamental fence keep my dog in?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>For most dogs, yes. Small dogs can slip between standard pickets, so ask about closer picket spacing or a mesh liner along the bottom.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Can an ornamental fence be a pool barrier?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes, in a pool-code style: at least 48 inches tall, no opening a 4-inch ball can pass through, and self-closing, self-latching gates. See <a href="/fence-styles/pool-enclosure/">pool enclosures</a>.</p></div></details>
</div>
