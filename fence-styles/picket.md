<h2>What it looks like</h2>
<p>A picket fence is a short fence with evenly spaced vertical pickets, usually three to four feet tall. It marks the edge of a yard, frames a garden or walkway, and keeps small pets and kids close without blocking the view of the house.</p>
<p>Picket tops can be flat, pointed, dog-eared, or scalloped, and the spacing can be tight or open. A matching walk gate finishes the look.</p>
</div>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Privacy level:</strong> Low privacy</li>
<li><strong>Typical height:</strong> 3&ndash;4 ft typical</li>
</ul>
</section>

<section class="section76-alt full-bleed76">

<h2>When a picket fence makes sense</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Front yards.</strong> A picket fence gives the house a finished edge without hiding it.</li>
<li><strong>Gardens and walkways.</strong> A short run keeps dogs out of the beds and frames the path to the door.</li>
<li><strong>Small pets and kids.</strong> Tight spacing keeps them close while you can still see the street.</li>
<li><strong>Farmhouse and country homes.</strong> A white or painted picket suits the look on acreage near Magnolia and Pinehurst.</li>
<li><strong>Not for privacy.</strong> For a closed-in backyard, see <a href="/fence-styles/privacy/">privacy fences</a>.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>

<h2>Acreage or subdivision lot?</h2>
<div class="cards76">
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On acreage</h3><p>On acreage, a picket fence is usually a finishing touch near the house &mdash; around a front yard, garden, or entry &mdash; rather than a boundary fence.</p></div></div>
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On subdivision lots</h3><p>On subdivision lots, picket fencing is mostly used in front yards and garden areas. Front-yard fences are the ones HOAs are most likely to restrict, so if your property has HOA or deed restrictions, check with your board first.</p></div></div>
</div>

<section class="section76">
<div class="prose76">
<h2>Local rules</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>City of Magnolia.</strong> Inside city limits, a fence needs a city permit: $65 plus a $50 administrative fee on the November 2024 schedule.</li>
<li><strong>City of Montgomery.</strong> Fences are on the city's list of projects that need a permit.</li>
<li><strong>Unincorporated Montgomery County.</strong> A typical residential fence usually doesn't need a county permit, but HOA or POA approval may still apply.</li>
<li><strong>Front yards.</strong> These are the fences HOAs restrict most, often by height, style, and color. We check your deed restrictions first.</li>
<li><strong>Around a pool.</strong> Most pool barrier codes call for at least 48 inches and no opening a 4-inch ball can pass through, so a 3-foot picket fence won't qualify, and a 4-foot one has to meet the full code. See <a href="/fence-styles/pool-enclosure/">pool enclosures</a>.</li>
</ul>
<p>See <a href="/service-areas/magnolia-tx/">fencing in Magnolia</a> and our <a href="/faq/fence-permits-magnolia-montgomery/">permit guide</a>.</p>
</div>

<h2>What affects the cost</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Material.</strong> On our <a href="/pricing-guide/">pricing guide</a>, cedar typically runs $30&ndash;$47 per linear foot installed, vinyl $25&ndash;$70, and aluminum and steel $40&ndash;$100.</li>
<li><strong>Design.</strong> Picket style, top shape, and spacing change the price.</li>
<li><strong>Finish.</strong> Painted or stained wood adds a finishing step.</li>
<li><strong>Gates.</strong> A matching walk gate and hardware are priced on their own.</li>
<li><strong>Length.</strong> Short runs cost more per foot, because corners and gates are spread over fewer feet.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2>Picket fence questions</h2>
<div class="faq76">
<details><summary>How tall is a picket fence?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Usually 3 to 4 feet. That's tall enough to mark the yard and keep small pets in, and short enough to keep the view open.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Wood, vinyl, or metal?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Painted wood has the classic look but needs repainting. Vinyl stays white with a rinse. Aluminum and steel give a cleaner, more formal look and hold up well.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Will a picket fence keep my dog in?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>For small and calm dogs, often yes, with tight spacing. Jumpers and diggers need something taller. See <a href="/fence-styles/pet/">pet fences</a>.</p></div></details>
</div>
