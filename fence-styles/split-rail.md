<div class="prose76">
<h2>What it looks like</h2>
<p>Split rail fences use rough-split rails set into mortised posts, usually two or three rails high, for a rustic, open look. Post-and-rail (or board) fencing uses milled boards fastened to square posts for a cleaner, more finished version of the same idea.</p>
<p>Most rail fences are three to four and a half feet tall. They mark a boundary without blocking the view, and they're often paired with welded wire or no-climb mesh on the inside when they also need to hold pets or livestock.</p>
</div>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Privacy level:</strong> No privacy &mdash; open view</li>
<li><strong>Typical height:</strong> 3&ndash;4.5 ft typical</li>
</ul>

<h2>Acreage or subdivision lot?</h2>
<div class="cards76">
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On acreage</h3><p>Rail fencing is built for acreage. It's the most popular choice along road frontage, driveways, and front pastures on the multi-acre lots around Magnolia, often with wire fencing across the back of the property.</p></div></div>
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On subdivision lots</h3><p>On subdivision lots, a short run of rail fencing can frame a front yard or driveway for a ranch-style look. If your property has HOA or deed restrictions, check with your board before adding a front-yard fence.</p></div></div>
</div>

<h2>Good to know</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li>Add wire mesh on the inside if the fence needs to hold dogs or livestock.</li>
<li>Strong end and corner posts keep long rail runs straight.</li>
<li>We request Texas 811 utility locates before any digging.</li>
<li>Wood rails benefit from sealing; vinyl rails skip that step.</li>
</ul>
