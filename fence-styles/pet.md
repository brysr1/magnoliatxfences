<h2>Built around your dog</h2>
<p>Most dogs get out one of three ways: through a gap, under the bottom, or over the top. A pet fence is a fence built or adapted to close off all three for your particular dog. A small terrier that can slip through a three-inch gap needs different spacing than a big, athletic dog that can clear four feet from a standstill.</p>
<p>Before we recommend a layout, we ask about your dog's size, breed, and any past escapes. Byron Dean, our General Manager, usually walks the yard and talks it through with you.</p>
</div>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Typical height:</strong> 4 to 6 ft, depending on the dog's size and jumping ability</li>
<li><strong>Gaps:</strong> tight board or picket spacing, or no-gap panels, for small dogs</li>
<li><strong>Digging:</strong> buried bottom rail, concrete mow strip, or L-footer wire</li>
<li><strong>Gates:</strong> self-closing, self-latching hardware so the yard stays secure if someone forgets</li>
</ul>

<h2>Local things to plan for</h2>
<p>Sandy topsoil over clay, common around Magnolia, is easy for dogs to dig and can settle after heavy rain, opening small gaps under a fence that's otherwise fine. A dig barrier along the base is cheap insurance.</p>
<p>Texas summers matter too. A dog that spends time outside needs shade and airflow along with a secure yard, so we'll talk through where shade falls and whether a little ventilation near the bottom of a solid fence makes sense. Some HOAs restrict chain link or require it to be screened from the street, so check your guidelines; see <a href="/hoa-fencing/">HOA fencing</a>. We request Texas 811 locates before any digging.</p>

<h2>Pet fence questions</h2>
<div class="faq76">
<details><summary>Can you keep my dog from digging under the fence?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes. A buried bottom rail, a concrete mow strip, or L-footer wire along the base are the usual fixes, and we recommend one based on how determined a digger your dog is.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>How tall should a fence be for a large, athletic dog?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Generally 6 feet for dogs that can clear 4 feet from a standstill, though it varies by breed. Check your HOA's height limit before deciding.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Do pet fences need a permit?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>The same rules apply as for any backyard fence. In unincorporated Montgomery County a typical residential fence usually doesn't need one, but HOA approval may. We confirm what applies to your address.</p></div></details>
