<h2>What it looks like</h2>
<p>A pool enclosure fence surrounds the pool area to keep children and pets out when no one is watching. Most are ornamental aluminum or steel, so the pool stays visible from the house and the fence doesn't block the view of the yard, lake, or golf course beyond it.</p>
<p>Pool barriers are usually at least four feet tall, with vertical pickets spaced closely enough that a small child can't slip through, and gates that close and latch on their own. The exact requirements depend on your local code, your HOA, and sometimes your insurer, so we confirm what applies to your property before we build.</p>
</div>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Privacy level:</strong> Low privacy, high visibility</li>
<li><strong>Typical height:</strong> 4 ft minimum typical; 5 ft common</li>
</ul>

<h2>When a pool fence makes sense</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>A new pool or spa.</strong> Plan the fence with the pool, so gates line up with paths and the barrier is ready when the pool is.</li>
<li><strong>A pool fenced only by the yard fence.</strong> A separate enclosure keeps the pool closed off even when a side gate is left open.</li>
<li><strong>Lake and golf lots.</strong> Open metal keeps the view of the water or fairway, which is what most architectural committees around Lake Conroe expect.</li>
<li><strong>Grandkids, visitors, and pets.</strong> A pool fence adds a layer of protection even if your own children are grown.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="prose76">
<h2>Local rules around Magnolia and Lake Conroe</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Inside city limits.</strong> Texas lets cities set their own pool fence standards (<a href="https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/LG/htm/LG.214.htm#214.101" rel="noopener">Local Government Code &sect;214.101</a>). Inside Magnolia, Montgomery, or Conroe, the city's code applies, and Magnolia and Montgomery both require a permit for fences.</li>
<li><strong>Unincorporated Montgomery County.</strong> A typical residential fence there usually doesn't need a county permit, but your pool builder, HOA or POA, and sometimes your insurer will still expect a code-style barrier.</li>
<li><strong>HOA and POA approval.</strong> Lake and golf communities like Bentwater, Walden, April Sound, and Grand Harbor have architectural committees that approve fences and pool barriers. Plan on approval before we start.</li>
<li><strong>Lake lots.</strong> On west-shore Lake Conroe lots, the back of the lot can run into land the San Jacinto River Authority controls. We keep the fence off it. See <a href="/faq/property-survey/">do you need a survey</a>.</li>
</ul>
<p>The barrier itself follows the same basic measurements in most codes: at least 48 inches tall, no opening a 4-inch ball can pass through, and walk gates that open away from the pool and close and latch on their own. The <a href="https://cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/362%20Safety%20Barrier%20Guidelines%20for%20Pools.pdf" rel="noopener">Consumer Product Safety Commission's barrier guidelines</a> explain the details. For the rules by city, see <a href="/service-areas/magnolia-tx/">fencing in Magnolia</a> and our <a href="/faq/fence-permits-magnolia-montgomery/">permit guide</a>.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section76-alt full-bleed76">
<div class="inner76">
<h2>What affects the cost</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Material.</strong> On our <a href="/pricing-guide/">pricing guide</a>, installed aluminum typically runs $40&ndash;$75 per linear foot and steel $50&ndash;$85.</li>
<li><strong>Length and layout.</strong> A fence around the pool deck is shorter than a full yard fence, but corners and curves add posts.</li>
<li><strong>Gates.</strong> Self-closing hinges and pool latches cost more than standard hardware, and each gate adds a frame.</li>
<li><strong>Ground.</strong> Slopes toward the lake, decking, and tree roots change how posts are set.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section76">
<h2>Good to know</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li>Self-closing, self-latching gates are the most important part of a pool fence.</li>
<li>Gates swing away from the pool and latch out of a small child's reach.</li>
<li>We request Texas 811 utility locates before any digging.</li>
<li>Aluminum resists the rust that pool chemicals and splash can cause.</li>
</ul>
</section>

<h2>Pool fence questions</h2>
<div class="faq76">
<details><summary>How tall does a pool fence need to be?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Most codes call for at least 48 inches, measured on the outside. Many owners choose 5 feet. If you're inside city limits, the city's code decides, and your HOA may set the style.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Do I need a permit for a pool fence?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Inside the City of Magnolia or Montgomery, fences need a city permit. In unincorporated Montgomery County, a typical residential fence usually doesn't need a county permit. Your HOA or POA may still need to approve it. We confirm which applies to your address.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Aluminum or steel around a pool?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Both work. Aluminum won't rust from splash and pool chemicals, which is why it's the most common choice here. Powder-coated steel is heavier and stronger, but scratches need a touch-up so rust doesn't start.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Which way should the gate swing?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Away from the pool. Walk gates should close and latch on their own, and a latch lower than 54 inches goes on the pool side, near the top of the gate.</p></div></details>
</div>
