<h2>The hardest-working part of the fence</h2>
<p>A gate is the one section of a fence with moving parts, and the one that gets pushed, pulled, and driven through every day. It also hangs its whole weight on two hinges instead of tying into a continuous line. That's why weak framing, undersized hinges, or a gate post that shifts shows up at the gate first.</p>
<p>We size every gate for what goes through it &mdash; foot traffic, a trash can, a riding mower, a truck and trailer &mdash; and set the gate posts deeper and wider than line posts, which matters in the sandy topsoil common around Magnolia.</p>
</div>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Walk gates:</strong> single gates sized for foot traffic, trash cans, and mowers</li>
<li><strong>Drive gates:</strong> single or double (dual-leaf), sized for vehicles, mowers, and trailers</li>
<li><strong>Openers:</strong> solar or wired automatic operators, with keypad or remote access</li>
</ul>

<h2>Automatic gate openers</h2>
<p>For daily vehicle access, especially on acreage entrances off FM 1488, FM 1774, and the county roads around Magnolia, an automatic opener saves getting out of the truck twice a day. We source and install both <strong>solar</strong> and <strong>wired</strong> operators for swing and slide gates.</p>
<p>A powered gate needs a sturdy gate and posts, a power source (a solar panel or an electrical run), an access method such as a keypad, remote, or phone app, and safety sensors so the gate stops if something is in the way. In many cases we can add an opener to an existing gate if it's strong enough to carry a motor.</p>
</div>
<div>
<h2>Wind, HOAs, and storm season</h2>
<p>A solid gate catches wind just like a privacy panel, but carries that load on its hinges and latch. On wide drive gates we size the hinges, posts, and bracing for the wind the panel will see, not just its weight.</p>
<p>In HOA neighborhoods, the gate's style and height are usually reviewed with the rest of the fence, and keypads or visible operator hardware can be a separate approval item. See <a href="/hoa-fencing/">HOA fencing</a> for how approval typically works.</p>

<h2>Gate questions</h2>
<div class="faq76">
<details><summary>Why do gates sag before the rest of the fence?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>A gate carries its own weight on hinges instead of being tied into the fence line, so weak framing, light hinges, or a shifting post shows up there first. Reinforced framing and properly set gate posts prevent it.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Do I need HOA approval for a new gate?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Usually it's covered under the same approval as the fence, but some HOAs review gate style, keypads, or opener hardware separately. We help put together whatever your board asks for.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Can you add an automatic opener to my existing gate?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Often, yes, as long as the gate and posts are sturdy enough to handle a motor. We check both before recommending an operator.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>My new gate doesn't latch like it did on day one. Is that normal?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes. New posts settle, and gates sometimes need a small adjustment afterward. Give us a call and we'll come adjust it free of charge.</p></div></details>
