## Working Within Community Design Guidelines

Neighborhoods like Magnolia Ridge typically set standards for fence height, materials, colors, and placement, especially for fences visible from the street or common areas. Getting those details right before construction saves time and avoids costly changes later. We review your lot and goals with you, recommend a style that fits within your community's rules, and provide the drawings and material details you need for your approval request.

Fence options we install in Magnolia Ridge include:

- **Wood privacy fences** in cedar or treated pine, available in side-by-side, board-on-board, or shadowbox styles
- **Stain and seal finishes** to match an approved neighborhood color and protect the wood
- **Ornamental iron and aluminum** for pool enclosures, front-yard accents, and open-view side yards
- **Gates** of every size, from a simple walk gate to a double drive gate for trailers or a boat

## Fences That Blend In

A good fence in a planned neighborhood shouldn't draw attention for the wrong reasons. When your project connects to an existing builder fence or a neighbor's fence, we match height, cap style, and spacing so the finished fence line looks uniform. That consistency is what HOAs look for, and it keeps Magnolia Ridge looking its best.

## Repairs That Extend the Life of Your Fence

Not every fence needs to be replaced. We regularly repair leaning sections, swap out broken pickets, reset posts, and rebuild sagging gates for homeowners in Magnolia Ridge. If a full replacement makes more sense, we'll show you why and quote a new fence in the same approved style.

<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>Permits and jurisdiction</h3><p>If your address is inside City of Magnolia limits, the city requires a fence permit ($65 plus a $50 administrative fee), and the application asks for the fence's length, material, and height.</p><p>Outside city limits, in unincorporated Montgomery County, a typical residential fence usually doesn't need a county building permit.</p><p>If the fence line crosses a mapped 100-year floodplain, the county can require a floodplain development permit; three-strand barbed wire fences are exempt.</p><p>HOA or POA approval is separate from any permit, and we request Texas 811 locates before digging.</p></div></div>
