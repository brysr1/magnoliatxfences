<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> On a typical subdivision lot with corner pins you can find, usually not. On acreage, on a shared line where the neighbor is unsure, or on a lake or fairway lot, a survey or freshly marked pins is worth the cost before a single post goes in.</p>
</div>
<h2>Where a survey earns its keep</h2>
<p>Most of the survey questions we get around Magnolia come from three kinds of property, and each has its own reason for caution.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Acreage tracts.</strong> On a five- or ten-acre parcel off FM 1774 or FM 149, a line that's off by a couple of degrees at one corner can drift several feet by the far end. Over a long run, that adds up to a lot of fence on the wrong side.</li>
<li><strong>Old fences that may not sit on the line.</strong> Barbed wire built decades ago often followed a tree line or dodged a wet draw. Building a new fence along the old one carries the old mistake forward.</li>
<li><strong>Lots with shared or contested lines.</strong> If you and the neighbor disagree about where the boundary is, a surveyor settles it before money goes into the ground, not after.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Finding the pins you already have</h2>
<p>Many lots in Audubon, North Grove, or Woodtrace still have their iron rods at the corners from when the subdivision was platted. They tend to disappear under pine straw, sod, or a flower bed. Start with your closing survey, which shows where each corner should be, then probe the spot with a metal detector or a long screwdriver. A capped rod with a surveyor's number stamped on it is the find you want.</p>
<p>If a corner is missing, a surveyor can reset it. That's usually cheaper than a full new survey and gives us something solid to measure from. Before you hire one, you can confirm the surveyor's registration on the <a href="https://pels.texas.gov/roster/ls_rosters.html" rel="noopener">state surveyor roster</a>.</p>
<h2>Plats, deed restrictions, and which county</h2>
<p>Your recorded plat shows the lot lines and the easements a fence has to respect, and recorded deed restrictions can set where a fence may sit. Our <a href="/resources/hoa-fence-approval-magnolia-tx/">HOA approval guide</a> notes that the Montgomery County Clerk keeps those records for land in this county.</p>
<p>One local wrinkle: <a href="/neighborhoods/clear-creek-forest/">Clear Creek Forest</a> straddles a county line. Sections 1 through 9 are in Waller County, while Sections 10 through 13 and the Estates of Clear Creek are in Montgomery County, which is the part we serve. If you're near that edge, make sure the plat and survey you're working from are for the right section.</p>
<p>An online parcel map is a handy starting point for finding your lot, but it isn't a survey. When the position of the fence matters, we build from pins or a survey, not from a map outline.</p>
<h2>Lake lots and the 201-foot line</h2>
<p>On the west side of Lake Conroe, in Bentwater, Walden, Grand Harbor, and Cape Conroe, the back of the lot can run into land the San Jacinto River Authority controls. SJRA's <a href="https://www.sjra.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Lake-Conroe-Rules-and-Regulations-2015.pdf" rel="noopener">Lake Conroe Rules and Regulations</a> define Authority Land to include ground up to the 201-foot contour above mean sea level (Section 1.02). An encroachment is a structure or fixture permanently placed there, and building one without an SJRA permit is a violation (Section 6.02). A set-in-concrete fence would most likely count; the rules exempt only items that can be removed immediately and certain landscaping and irrigation (Section 6.04).</p>
<p>A survey that shows that contour, not just your deed boundary, tells us where the fence should end unless SJRA approves more. Fairway lots have a similar issue with the golf course boundary, plus whatever setback the architectural committee requires. The low end of a lake lot can also sit in a flood zone; see our <a href="/faq/flood-zone-easement-fencing/">flood zone and easement FAQ</a>.</p>
<h2>What we do at the estimate</h2>
<p>We look for pins, compare them with your plat or survey, and tell you plainly whether we're comfortable building from what's there. If something doesn't add up, we'll say so and hold off staking until it's sorted. On a shared line, once the pins are settled we can quote the run so each neighbor sees their share; the cost and consent questions are covered in <a href="/faq/property-line-fence-cost/">who pays for a property line fence</a>. Our <a href="/acreage-ranch-fencing/">acreage fencing</a> page shows how we lay out long runs from the corners, and the <a href="/pricing-guide/">pricing guide</a> lists typical per-foot ranges.</p>
