<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> No single material wins on every count here. Ornamental aluminum handles damp air and lakeside exposure best, open metal and chain link handle wind best, and cedar on well-set posts is still the best value for full privacy. The ground under a fence decides its life as much as the material on top.</p>
</div>
<h2>Four things our weather throws at a fence</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Sun and heat.</strong> At the Conroe station, the closest long-term record to most of our jobs, NOAA's <a href="https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/us-climate-normals/#dataset=normals-monthly&amp;timeframe=30&amp;location=TX&amp;station=USC00411956" rel="noopener">1991&ndash;2020 climate normals</a> put the average July high at 94.9&deg;F and August at 95.8&deg;F. That fades stain, dries out wood, and makes vinyl expand and contract through the day.</li>
<li><strong>Long wet spells.</strong> The same normals give about 51 inches of rain a year, with May, June, and October each averaging more than 5 inches. Rain often arrives in bunches and keeps the bottom of a fence damp for days.</li>
<li><strong>Storm wind and falling limbs.</strong> Tropical systems and spring storms push on solid panels, and on wooded lots a dropped pine limb does more damage than the wind itself.</li>
<li><strong>Sand over clay.</strong> The USDA's <a href="https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/C/CONROE.html" rel="noopener">Conroe series description</a>, the county's most common soil, shows loamy fine sand on top and slow-draining clay with ironstone starting about 23 to 43 inches down. Sand gives a post little side grip, and water can sit on that layer inside a post hole.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Your deed restrictions may choose for you</h2>
<p>Before weighing weather, check what your neighborhood allows. <a href="/neighborhoods/ranch-crest/">Ranch Crest's</a> Sections III and IV allow wood, brick, wrought iron, and vinyl but not chain link or wire, and wood there may be sealed but not painted. <a href="/neighborhoods/westwood/">Westwood's</a> 2016 guidelines allow wood, chain link, wrought iron, or vinyl, and its original Section 2 restrictions say wood fences must be painted or stained. In <a href="/neighborhoods/woodforest/">Woodforest</a>, a replacement fence has to be similar in material and design to the original. Our <a href="/resources/hoa-fence-approval-magnolia-tx/">HOA approval guide</a> covers the paperwork.</p>
<h2>Matching the material to the property</h2>
<p>On acreage outside Magnolia and Pinehurst, the usual mix is wire across the pasture, rail along the road, and wood or vinyl privacy only near the house. Wire fences live or die by their corners, so put the money into H-braces before a heavier wire; our <a href="/materials/farm-ranch/">farm and ranch materials</a> page covers the options.</p>
<p>Along the west shore of Lake Conroe, in Bentwater, April Sound, Walden, and their neighbors, damp air off the water and sprinkler overspray favor aluminum, and the view rules usually do too. On subdivision lots where privacy is the point, a well-built cedar fence remains the most common answer. Under heavy pine and oak shade, as in <a href="/neighborhoods/cimarron-country/">Cimarron Country</a> or <a href="/neighborhoods/clear-creek-forest/">Clear Creek Forest</a>, wood stays damp longer and termites find it easier; see our <a href="/resources/termite-damage-wood-fence/">termite guide</a>. Our <a href="/faq/best-fence-for-wind/">wind page</a> covers which styles shed a gust and which ones catch it.</p>
<h2>What no material can make up for</h2>
<p>Most of the fence failures we're called to fix start at the posts, not the pickets or panels. We set every post in concrete, a minimum of 24 inches down with 80 lb in each hole on a wood fence, adding depth for taller fences and wherever we hit loose sand or a hole that fills with water. Posts that hang a gate or turn a corner go deeper still, sized to the gate and the soil. Our wood fences also get three rails, a 6-inch rot board, Simpson brackets, and hot-dipped galvanized ring-shank nails. Our <a href="/resources/fence-posts-montgomery-county-soil/">post-setting guide for local soil</a> explains how we decide hole by hole.</p>
