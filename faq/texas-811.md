<div class="callout76">
<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Yes. Texas law requires a locate request before mechanized digging, and power-auguring fence post holes counts. We request it before every new fence we install, and the crew waits until the marks are down before the first hole goes in.</p>
</div>
<h2>What Texas law counts as digging</h2>
<p>The rules live in Chapter 251 of the Texas Utilities Code. It defines excavation as using powered or mechanized equipment, augers included, to disturb soil 16 inches deep or more. Our wood-fence post holes go at least 24 inches, and gate and corner holes go deeper, so a new fence is squarely covered. Texas811 lists <a href="https://texas811.org/?p=2965" rel="noopener">fence projects</a> among those that need a request, even in your own backyard.</p>
<h2>How the timing works on a Magnolia job</h2>
<p>Under <a href="https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/UT/htm/UT.251.htm#251.151" rel="noopener">Section 251.151</a>, the request has to go in at least 48 hours before digging, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or legal holidays, and no earlier than 14 days ahead. In practice we place it after the layout is settled and before the install date, so the window lines up with the crew's schedule.</p>
<ul>
<li>A request made on a Thursday clears for digging at the same hour the following Monday, or Tuesday if Monday is a legal holiday.</li>
<li>The statute treats Saturday differently: a Saturday request clears at 11:59 a.m. the following Tuesday, or Wednesday if Monday is a holiday.</li>
<li>If rain, a permit, or an HOA review pushes the start more than 14 days past the request, we file a new one. That happens more than people expect when a City of Magnolia permit and an architectural committee review are running at the same time.</li>
<li>If marks disappear before we dig, we ask for a refresh rather than guessing.</li>
</ul>
<p>Once the flags are out, we hand-dig near any mark. A common practice is to treat 18 inches on each side of a mark as a hand-dig zone; Texas811's <a href="https://texas811.org/?p=3735" rel="noopener">excavator FAQ</a> describes that as an industry best practice. A post can usually shift a few inches along the line to stay clear.</p>
<h2>Lines 811 won't find on acreage and lake lots</h2>
<p>Chapter 251 doesn't apply to an underground line that serves only the owner and sits entirely on the owner's property (<a href="https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/UT/htm/UT.251.htm#251.003" rel="noopener">Section 251.003</a>). In plain terms, a utility marks what it owns, which generally stops at the meter. Anything that runs from there onto your land is yours, and on the properties we work around Magnolia that can be a lot of buried line:</p>
<ul>
<li>Electric run out to a barn, shop, well pump, or RV pad</li>
<li>Water lines to stock tanks and hose bibs at the far end of a pasture</li>
<li>Septic tanks, field lines, and aerobic spray systems. In <a href="/neighborhoods/high-meadow-estates/">High Meadow Estates</a>, for example, there's no MUD and lots typically use septic.</li>
<li>Power to a boat lift, dock lights, or a pool on a west Lake Conroe lot</li>
<li>Sprinkler mains, landscape lighting, and wiring for an existing gate opener</li>
</ul>
<p>Water and sewer lines have their own wrinkle. The statute requires operators of Class A facilities, such as gas and electric lines, to participate in a notification center (<a href="https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/UT/htm/UT.251.htm#251.107" rel="noopener">Section 251.107</a>). Water and sewer are Class B, and that duty doesn't extend to them. If a MUD or small water company serves your street, as Montgomery County MUDs 8 and 9 do in Walden, a separate call can be worth it. For private electric or water runs, a private locating service can mark them for a fee.</p>
<h2>What helps before we arrive</h2>
<p>Walk the fence line and flag anything you know is buried, even if you're not sure how deep it runs. Tell us about old trenching you remember, a shop that was wired after the house was built, or a spot where the grass grows in a strange straight line. On acreage, point out the well house and where the septic field drains. If you aren't sure where your corners are, sort that out first; our <a href="/faq/property-survey/">property survey FAQ</a> covers finding pins. Then leave the 811 paint and flags alone until the posts are set.</p>
