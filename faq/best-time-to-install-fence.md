<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Any month works for the fence itself. What changes through the year is how long you wait for a spot on the schedule and how ready your lot is. Plan around HOA and permit lead time first, then the ground, then the calendar.</p>
</div>
<h2>How the year usually runs</h2>
<p>Spring into early summer is when our schedule fills fastest. Late summer through winter tends to be calmer, so a fence ordered in October or January often goes in sooner than the same fence ordered in April.</p>
<p>Rain doesn't take a season off here. NOAA's <a href="https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/us-climate-normals/#dataset=normals-monthly&amp;timeframe=30&amp;location=TX&amp;station=USC00411956" rel="noopener">1991&ndash;2020 normals for Conroe</a> show May, June, and October each averaging more than 5 inches, while February, March, April, and July average closer to 3.3 to 3.5. August is the hottest month, with an average high near 96&deg;F. None of that stops a fence, but it does move individual days. On our sandy-over-clay ground, post holes can hold water for a while after a storm, and a wet hole needs extra work before concrete goes in.</p>
<h2>Work back from your date</h2>
<p>If the fence has to be up for something specific, like a new puppy, a pool inspection, a closing, or holiday guests, count backward:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Estimate and quote.</strong> Book the estimate first, so you have a drawing and materials list for the HOA. Estimates are free.</li>
<li><strong>HOA or architectural review.</strong> Every committee runs on its own clock. <a href="/neighborhoods/walden-on-lake-conroe/">Walden's</a> committee meets every other Tuesday, with requests due by noon the Friday before. <a href="/neighborhoods/westwood/">Westwood's</a> guidelines give its committee 30 days to respond. <a href="/neighborhoods/high-meadow-estates/">High Meadow Estates</a> won't review an incomplete package. Audubon, Woodforest, and the lake communities have their own processes, and a missing page can cost weeks.</li>
<li><strong>City permit, if you're inside city limits.</strong> The City of Magnolia says approval usually takes about two weeks, and most permits expire 180 days after they're issued. We're glad to apply where the city allows it, and when the homeowner has to submit, we help prepare the paperwork and drawings. If we apply and pay the fee, it's passed through to you. See <a href="/faq/fence-permits-magnolia-montgomery/">our permit FAQ</a> for Montgomery, Conroe, and county lots.</li>
<li><strong>811 locates.</strong> Before a new install, we place the request at least 48 hours before digging, not counting weekends or legal holidays, and no more than 14 days ahead, as <a href="https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/UT/htm/UT.251.htm#251.151" rel="noopener">Section 251.151</a> of the Utilities Code requires. If an HOA or permit delay pushes the start past that window, we file a new one. Our <a href="/faq/texas-811/">811 FAQ</a> has the details.</li>
<li><strong>Installation.</strong> Most residential fences take one to three days on site.</li>
</ol>
<p>Pool barriers deserve the most cushion, since a pool often can't pass final inspection until the barrier is up. On a new build, start the HOA application before closing if your builder and HOA allow it; our <a href="/resources/new-construction-fence-magnolia-tx/">new-construction guide</a> covers the rest.</p>
<h2>Acreage timing</h2>
<p>On five or ten acres outside Magnolia, ground access is the bigger factor. A pasture that's soft after a wet stretch can rut badly under a loaded truck and auger, and low corners along a creek may stay wet for days. Late summer is often the easiest window for long runs, since July and August average less rain than May and June and the ground has usually firmed up. If you're clearing brush along the line, getting that done first makes the fence go faster. Our <a href="/acreage-ranch-fencing/">acreage fencing</a> page covers the line walk.</p>
<h2>Replacing an old fence</h2>
<p>If an old fence has to come out first, tear-out and haul-away are listed on the quote where they apply. They aren't part of the standard per-foot price of a new fence, so compare quotes on that line. If we find something on the day that changes the scope, like buried concrete or extra footage, we confirm it with you before adding any cost. If the up-front number is what's holding you back, <a href="/financing/">financing through Hearth</a> is available.</p>
<h2>Plan the stain separately</h2>
<p>A new wood fence shouldn't be stained the week it's built. The wood has to dry out first, and during humid stretches that takes longer. Many owners build in spring or summer and stain in the fall, when temperatures are milder and the finish soaks in instead of flashing off a hot board. Fall still brings some of our wettest weeks, so we schedule the <a href="/fence-staining-power-washing/">staining</a> as a second visit around a dry forecast once the boards are ready.</p>
