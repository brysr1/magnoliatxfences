<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Any month works for the fence itself. What changes through the year is how long you wait for a spot on the schedule and how ready your lot is. Plan around HOA and permit lead time first, then the ground, then the calendar.</p>
</div>
<h2>How the year usually runs</h2>
<p>Spring into early summer is when our schedule fills fastest. Late summer through winter tends to be calmer, so a fence ordered in October or January often goes in sooner than the same fence ordered in April.</p>
<p>Weather mostly moves individual days. A soaking rain can push a start back, and on our sandy-over-clay ground, post holes can hold water for a while after a storm, and a wet hole needs extra work before concrete goes in.</p>
<h2>Work back from your date</h2>
<p>If the fence has to be up for something specific, like a new puppy, a pool inspection, a closing, or holiday guests, count backward:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Estimate and quote.</strong> Book the estimate first, so you have a drawing and materials list for the HOA.</li>
<li><strong>HOA or architectural review.</strong> Committees in neighborhoods like Audubon and Woodforest, and in the lake communities, review on their own schedules, and an incomplete packet can cost weeks.</li>
<li><strong>City permit, if you're inside city limits.</strong> The City of Magnolia says approval usually takes about two weeks. See <a href="/faq/fence-permits-magnolia-montgomery/">our permit FAQ</a> for other jurisdictions.</li>
<li><strong>811 locates.</strong> At least two working days before digging.</li>
<li><strong>Installation.</strong> Most residential fences take one to three days on site.</li>
</ol>
<p>Pool barriers deserve the most cushion, since a pool often can't pass final inspection until the barrier is up.</p>
<h2>Acreage timing</h2>
<p>On five or ten acres outside Magnolia, ground access is the bigger factor. A pasture that's soft after a wet stretch can rut badly under a loaded truck and auger, and low corners along a creek may stay wet for days. Late summer and fall, when the ground firms up, are often easier for long runs. If you're clearing brush along the line, getting that done first makes the fence go faster. Our <a href="/acreage-ranch-fencing/">acreage fencing</a> page covers the line walk.</p>
<h2>Plan the stain separately</h2>
<p>A new wood fence shouldn't be stained the week it's built. The wood has to dry out first, and during humid stretches that takes longer. Many owners build in spring or summer and stain in the fall, when the air is drier and milder. We can schedule the <a href="/fence-staining-power-washing/">staining</a> as a second visit once the boards are ready.</p>
