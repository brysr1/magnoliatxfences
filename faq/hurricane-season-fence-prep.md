<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Hurricane season in the Atlantic lasts from June 1 to November 30. Early in the season, fix leaning posts, loose boards, and gates that won't latch, and trim limbs hanging over the fence. When a storm is forecast, secure gates and loose yard items, then photograph the fence. Afterward, document any damage before you clean up.</p>
</div>
<h2>What storms have done here</h2>
<p>On wooded Magnolia-area lots, trees often do more damage to a fence than the wind does, and they're the bigger safety risk too. The National Hurricane Center's <a href="https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/tcr/AL092008_Ike.pdf" rel="noopener">report on Ike</a> notes a death in Montgomery County from a tree falling onto an occupied house in 2008. Its <a href="https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/tcr/AL022024_Beryl.pdf" rel="noopener">report on Beryl</a> attributes five Texas deaths to trees blown down by wind and six more to tree trimming and clearing after the storm, and it cites media reports of 2.7 million people without power in southeastern Texas. Plan your season around the trees on your lot and the chance of a long outage.</p>
<h2>Early in the season: a walk-around</h2>
<p>June is the time to find the weak spots, while repairs can still be scheduled without a storm on the way. Walk the whole fence and push on it every few sections.</p>
<ul>
<li>Posts that rock or lean, especially at gates and corners</li>
<li>Pickets with popped nails or boards separating from the rails</li>
<li>A rot board that's soft or crumbling along the bottom</li>
<li>Gates that drag, sag, or need lifting to latch</li>
<li>Dead pines or heavy limbs over the fence line; call an arborist for big trees</li>
<li>On acreage, slack wire and corner braces that have shifted</li>
<li>On an automatic drive gate, the manual release key, and whether you know how to use it</li>
</ul>
<p>A single leaning post is a small repair in June. In a 60 mph gust, about what the Conroe airport recorded during Ike, it can pull down the sections on either side. If you find more than you want to handle, our <a href="/maintenance-repair/">repair crew</a> can take care of it before the season gets busy.</p>
<h2>When a storm is in the forecast</h2>
<ol>
<li>Close and latch every gate. Tie off any with a weak latch.</li>
<li>Put an automatic drive gate in a position you can live with if the power goes out. Solar operators keep running on their batteries, and wired ones need a backup battery. Our <a href="/resources/automatic-gate-opener-acreage/">acreage gate opener guide</a> covers both.</li>
<li>Move trampolines, patio furniture, trash cans, and anything else the wind could throw against the fence.</li>
<li>On acreage, move horses or cattle away from tree lines and wire you already know is weak, if you have a better pasture to put them in.</li>
<li>Take a few wide photos of the fence from both sides. Pictures of its condition before the storm help if you file a claim later.</li>
</ol>
<h2>After it passes</h2>
<p>Wait until it's safe, and stay clear of downed power lines tangled in a fence. Leave any tree that's large, hung up, or under tension to a tree crew, given the deaths from post-storm tree clearing after Beryl. Then photograph damaged sections, leaning posts, and any tree on the fence before anything is moved. Our guide to <a href="/resources/fence-storm-damage-insurance-claim/">fence storm damage claims</a> covers what an insurer usually wants to see.</p>
<p>TDI's <a href="https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/storms/home-damaged-faq.html" rel="noopener">storm damage FAQ</a> says a fence is likely covered if your policy covers wind, usually at actual cash value, meaning depreciated, with your deductible applied. If your policy pays replacement cost, the first check is often actual cash value, and the rest typically comes after you make the repair and send proof. Home policies don't cover flood, which matters for lakefront and low-lying lots. And because <a href="https://www.twia.org/?p=40566" rel="noopener">TWIA's coverage area</a> doesn't include Montgomery County, there's no separate windstorm policy in the picture here. Check your own policy or call your agent, since this is general information and not insurance advice.</p>

<p>If a section is down and you have pets or livestock, prop or tie it temporarily rather than leaving a gap. Livestock panels, T-posts and wire, or a gate tied shut will hold until the repair. On waterfront lots in Bentwater, April Sound, or Walden, check the lakeside fence once the water comes back down, since high water can soften the ground around posts near the shoreline.</p>
