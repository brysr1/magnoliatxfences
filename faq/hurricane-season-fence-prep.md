<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Hurricane season in the Atlantic lasts from June 1 to November 30. Early in the season, fix leaning posts, loose boards, and gates that won't latch, and trim limbs hanging over the fence. When a storm is forecast, secure gates and loose yard items, then photograph the fence. Afterward, document any damage before you clean up.</p>
</div>
<h2>Early in the season: a walk-around</h2>
<p>June is the time to find the weak spots, while repairs can still be scheduled without a storm on the way. Walk the whole fence and push on it every few sections.</p>
<ul>
<li>Posts that rock or lean, especially at gates and corners</li>
<li>Pickets with popped nails or boards separating from the rails</li>
<li>A rot board that's soft or crumbling along the bottom</li>
<li>Gates that drag, sag, or need lifting to latch</li>
<li>Dead pines or heavy limbs over the fence line; call an arborist for big trees</li>
<li>On acreage, slack wire and corner braces that have shifted</li>
</ul>
<p>A single leaning post is a small repair in June. In a 60 mph gust it can pull down the sections on either side. If you find more than you want to handle, our <a href="/maintenance-repair/">repair crew</a> can take care of it before the season gets busy.</p>
<h2>When a storm is in the forecast</h2>
<ol>
<li>Close and latch every gate. Tie off any with a weak latch.</li>
<li>Put an automatic drive gate in a position you can live with if the power goes out, and make sure you know how its manual release works.</li>
<li>Move trampolines, patio furniture, trash cans, and anything else the wind could throw against the fence.</li>
<li>Take a few wide photos of the fence from both sides. Pictures of its condition before the storm help if you file a claim later.</li>
</ol>
<h2>After it passes</h2>
<p>Wait until it's safe, and stay clear of downed power lines tangled in a fence. Then photograph damaged sections, leaning posts, and any tree on the fence before anything is moved. Our guide to <a href="/resources/fence-storm-damage-insurance-claim/">fence storm damage claims</a> covers what an insurer usually wants to see.</p>
<p>The Texas Department of Insurance notes that most home policies cover other structures, fences included, at 10% of the dwelling's insured value, and wind damage is typically paid at depreciated value after the deductible. Home policies don't cover flood, which matters for lakefront and low-lying lots. Check your own policy or call your agent, since this is general information and not insurance advice. TDI's <a href="https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/storms/home-damaged-faq.html" rel="noopener">storm damage FAQ</a> is a good starting point.</p>
<p>If a section is down and you have pets or livestock, prop or tie it temporarily rather than leaving a gap. On waterfront lots in Bentwater, April Sound, or Walden, check the lakeside fence once the water comes back down, since high water can soften the ground around posts near the shoreline.</p>
