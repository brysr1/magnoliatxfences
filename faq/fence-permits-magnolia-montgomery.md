<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Inside the City of Magnolia, yes, and inside the City of Montgomery, yes; fences are on that city's permit list. Outside city limits, including all of Pinehurst, the county generally doesn't permit an ordinary backyard fence, though a lot in a mapped floodplain is an exception. HOA approval is a separate question either way.</p>

<p>The area we serve is a patchwork. Magnolia and Montgomery are small cities surrounded by unincorporated county. Pinehurst is a community with no city government. Some west Lake Conroe neighborhoods fall under the City of Conroe, and others sit in a city's extraterritorial jurisdiction without being inside it. We check the jurisdiction for every address before we quote. Here's what we know about each:</p>

<p>We don't publish a height limit for Magnolia or Montgomery here. The numbers live in each city's code, codes get amended, and a corner lot or front-yard rule can change the answer. We confirm the limit for your lot.</p>
<h2>Lots near the lines</h2>
<p>A few situations catch people off guard around here:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>FM 1488 and FM 1774 frontage.</strong> Neighborhoods just outside Magnolia's limits look the same as those inside. A tax statement with no City of Magnolia line usually means no city fence permit.</li>
<li><strong>ETJ neighborhoods.</strong> <a href="/neighborhoods/audubon/">Audubon</a> is in Magnolia's ETJ, and Montgomery County MUD 18 describes <a href="/neighborhoods/bentwater/">Bentwater</a> as being in Conroe's ETJ rather than inside the city. ETJ is not city limits, so we confirm what reaches the lot instead of guessing.</li>
<li><strong>Annexation plans change.</strong> <a href="/neighborhoods/carriage-hills/">Carriage Hills</a> is unincorporated; Conroe removed it from its long-term annexation plan in 2018. April Sound went the other way and has been in the city since 2015.</li>
<li><strong>Creek acreage.</strong> A tract that backs to Mill Creek, Lake Creek, or Spring Creek may be outside any city but still inside the county's mapped floodplain. Montgomery County's <a href="https://cms1files.revize.com/montgomerycountytx/Development%20Regulations%20-%20Adopted%203-4-2025.pdf" rel="noopener">development regulations</a> (Section 5) say all development in the floodplain must meet the county's floodplain rules and get permitting from its Floodplain Administration Department. The <a href="/faq/flood-zone-easement-fencing/">flood zone FAQ</a> covers what that means for a fence.</li>
</ul>
<h2>When a new section goes in behind you</h2>
<p>Much of the growth along FM 1488 and FM 1774 is new subdivisions built next to older lots and acreage. The same county regulations, in Section 3(C)(18), require a new single-family development that shares a property line with existing homes to provide a privacy fence at least six feet tall along that line, built so it doesn't block drainage from neighboring land. If a new section is platted behind you, ask the county or the developer whether that fence is planned before you pay for one on the same line.</p>
<h2>HOA approval is its own yes</h2>
<p>A city permit and a homeowners association approval answer different questions. The city checks code; the HOA, POA, or architectural committee checks your deed restrictions on height, material, color, and placement. Lake and golf communities in particular review fences closely, and an approval from one doesn't stand in for the other. Our <a href="/resources/hoa-fence-approval-magnolia-tx/">HOA approval guide</a> walks through the packet, and the <a href="/hoa-fencing/">HOA fencing page</a> covers what committees usually ask for.</p>
<h2>How we handle the paperwork</h2>
<p>At the estimate we tell you which approvals your address needs. Where a city lets a contractor apply, we're happy to file, and any permit fee we pay is passed through to you. Where the office wants the owner to submit, we still prepare the drawing, footage, height, and material details so the application goes in complete. Permit fees are separate from the per-foot ranges in our <a href="/pricing-guide/">pricing guide</a>, which lists them among the things that move a final price.</p>

The city permit, an HOA review, and the <a href="/faq/texas-811/">Texas 811 locate</a> each run on their own clock, and an 811 request can't go in more than 14 days before digging, so we place it once the approvals are settled.
