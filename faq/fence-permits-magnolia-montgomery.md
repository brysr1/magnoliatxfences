<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Inside the City of Magnolia, yes, and inside the City of Montgomery, plan on checking with the city first. Outside city limits, including all of Pinehurst, the county generally doesn't permit an ordinary backyard fence, though a lot in a mapped floodplain is an exception. HOA approval is a separate question either way.</p>
</div>
<h2>Start with who governs the lot</h2>
<p>The area we serve is a patchwork. Magnolia and Montgomery are small cities surrounded by unincorporated county, Pinehurst is a census-designated community with no city government, and some west Lake Conroe neighborhoods fall under the City of Conroe. We check the jurisdiction for every address before we quote. Here's what we know about each:</p>

<p>We don't publish a height limit for Magnolia or Montgomery here. The numbers live in each city's code, codes get amended, and some lots have a corner or front-yard rule that changes the answer. We confirm the limit for your lot.</p>
<h2>Lots near the lines</h2>
<p>A few situations catch people off guard. Neighborhoods just outside the Magnolia city limits along FM 1488 or FM 1774 look the same as those inside. Bentwater sits in Conroe's extraterritorial jurisdiction rather than inside its limits, according to the MUD that serves it. And acreage that backs to a creek may be outside any city but still inside the county's mapped floodplain; the <a href="/faq/flood-zone-easement-fencing/">flood zone FAQ</a> covers that permit.</p>
<h2>HOA approval is its own yes</h2>
<p>A city permit and a homeowners association approval answer different questions. The city checks code; the HOA, POA, or architectural committee checks your deed restrictions on height, material, color, and placement. Lake and golf communities in particular review fences closely, and an approval from one doesn't stand in for the other. Our <a href="/hoa-fencing/">HOA fencing page</a> covers what committees usually ask for.</p>
