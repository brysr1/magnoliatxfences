<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Whoever wants the fence usually pays for it, unless both owners agree to share. Texas has no statewide rule dividing the bill, but it does limit tearing out a fence two owners share. This page is general information, not legal advice.</p>
</div>
<h2>A split is a private deal</h2>
<p>Around Magnolia, the split most often comes up when a builder fence between two backyards starts to lean, or when a new acreage owner wants to replace a sagging wire fence with the neighbor's pasture on the other side. In both cases nothing in state law sets the shares. Neighbors agree to 50/50, to one paying for materials and the other for labor, or to one owner covering it all and keeping control of the style.</p>
<p>Deed restrictions in a subdivision can add rules about who may build on a lot line, what it looks like, and which side faces out, so read yours before the conversation.</p>
<h2>The shared-fence rule in Ag Code 143</h2>
<p>Chapter 143 of the Texas Agriculture Code doesn't make anyone contribute, but Sections 143.121 through 143.123 deal with a fence two owners hold jointly, or a fence someone else has tied into. An owner can't remove that kind of dividing fence without the other owner's consent or six months' written notice. On acreage, where the neighbor's cattle or horses may depend on that wire, it matters. Pulling the old fence on a Saturday to rebuild it can leave the next pasture open.</p>
<p>Montgomery County also adopted stock laws decades ago, including one covering cattle countywide, which is one reason livestock owners tend to be the ones who want a tight boundary fence. Stock law coverage can vary, so the County Clerk is the place to confirm.</p>
<h2>Write the agreement down</h2>
<p>A short signed note avoids most of the arguments we hear about later. Cover:</p>
<ul>
<li>Each owner's share, and when each payment is due</li>
<li>Material, height, style, and which side gets the finished face</li>
<li>Exactly where the line runs, ideally tied to pins or a <a href="/faq/property-survey/">survey</a></li>
<li>Who maintains, stains, and repairs it, and how storm damage is split</li>
<li>What happens if one house sells</li>
</ul>
<h2>Building a few inches inside your line</h2>
<p>Some owners skip the negotiation and set the fence slightly inside their own boundary. The fence is then yours outright, and you choose the design. Leaving a narrow strip outside the fence doesn't by itself give it away. Texas courts have treated a fence placed for convenience, or built mainly to hold livestock, as weak evidence of a boundary claim on its own. Adverse possession has its own time limits and conditions, so a real estate attorney is the right call if a strip is in dispute.</p>
