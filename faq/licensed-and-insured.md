<div class="callout76">
<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Insured, yes. Licensed is a different matter: Texas doesn't issue a state license for fence contractors, or for general contractors. Ask for a certificate of insurance instead, and if the job includes a powered gate, ask who is doing the electrical work.</p>
</div>
<h2>Why a license number won't settle it</h2>
<p>Texas once had builder registration through the Texas Residential Construction Commission, but the state's Sunset Commission records that the agency was <a href="https://www.sunset.texas.gov/reviews-and-reports/agencies/texas-residential-construction-commission" rel="noopener">abolished on September 1, 2009</a>. Nothing replaced it for fencing. A company calling itself licensed may have a local registration, a sales tax permit, or nothing at all, and none of those say much about how the fence will be built.</p>
<p>What a permit office checks is the project, not the builder. Inside the City of Magnolia, the fence permit application asks for footage, material, and height. Inside the City of Montgomery, fences are on the city's list of projects that need a permit. April Sound sits inside the City of Conroe, so Conroe's rules apply there. In unincorporated Montgomery County, which covers Pinehurst and most acreage around Magnolia, there's usually no county permit for a typical fence, so the homeowner is the only one vetting the contractor. Our <a href="/faq/fence-permits-magnolia-montgomery/">permit FAQ</a> covers each jurisdiction.</p>
<h2>The two policies that matter</h2>
<p><strong>General liability</strong> pays when the contractor damages something that isn't the fence: a broken irrigation line, a cracked driveway from a loaded truck, a neighbor's car, a bulkhead or dock rail on a lake lot.</p>
<p><strong>Workers' compensation</strong> covers the crew if someone gets hurt on your property. The Texas Department of Insurance says most private employers <a href="https://www.tdi.texas.gov/wc/employer/index.html" rel="noopener">can choose whether to carry it</a>, and employers without it are called non-subscribers. That's legal, but you'd want to know it before an auger is running in your backyard.</p>
<p>When you ask for proof, have the certificate sent from the contractor's insurance agent rather than accepting a photo. Check that the company name matches the quote and that the policy dates cover your install. We carry general liability and workers' compensation, and we send certificates on request.</p>
<h2>Gate openers bring in a real license</h2>
<p>Electrical work is one area where Texas does license the trade. The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation says <a href="https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/elec.htm" rel="noopener">non-exempt electrical work must be licensed</a>, and a company doing it needs a master electrician of record. Running 120- or 240-volt power out to a drive gate at the end of a long acreage driveway off FM 1488 or FM 1774 is generally that kind of work.</p>
<p>TDLR's <a href="https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ELECTRICIANS/exemptions.htm" rel="noopener">exemptions page</a> lists a few situations that matter around here:</p>
<ul>
<li>Class 1, 2, or 3 remote control, signaling, or power-limited circuits, which can include low-voltage keypad and sensor wiring</li>
<li>Work on a home the owner both owns and lives in</li>
<li>Electrical work on buildings, structures, or equipment in agricultural use</li>
<li>Well pumps and their equipment</li>
</ul>
<p>The same page notes that city or regional rules can override those exemptions, so a lot inside the City of Magnolia or Montgomery may be treated differently from a tract in the county. Whether your gate fits an exemption is a question for a licensed electrician, not a fence crew.</p>
<p>A solar operator often sidesteps the question, which is part of why it's popular on long acreage driveways where the house is hundreds of feet from the gate. If you want a wired opener, ask who will run the power feed and confirm they're licensed. Our <a href="/fence-styles/gates/">gates and openers page</a> covers both types, and our <a href="/resources/automatic-gate-opener-acreage/">acreage gate opener guide</a> goes further.</p>
<h2>Other questions worth asking</h2>
<ul>
<li>Is the crew on site your own, or a subcontractor, and does your insurance cover them?</li>
<li>Who requests the 811 locate, and who handles the permit or HOA paperwork? We're happy to apply for a permit where the city allows it, and we help prepare the drawings either way.</li>
<li>What does the written warranty cover, and for how long? Ours is an 18-month limited workmanship warranty on fence runs we build ourselves; details are on the <a href="/warranty/">warranty page</a>.</li>
</ul>
