<div class="callout76">
<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Start with your dog's habits, then fit the material to your lot. A 6-foot board-on-board or solid vinyl fence handles jumpers, squeezers, and fence-line barkers in most backyards. Diggers need something below grade, and on acreage, no-climb wire covers a lot of ground for far less than privacy fencing.</p>
</div>
<h2>Match the fence to how your dog gets out</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Diggers.</strong> Sandy topsoil around Magnolia is easy digging, and heavy rain can wash out small gaps under an otherwise sound fence. Buried wire, an L-shaped wire footer, or a concrete mow strip along the base stops most of it.</li>
<li><strong>Jumpers.</strong> A dog that clears 4 feet from a standstill needs 6 feet. Avoid horizontal rails or brackets on the dog's side that work as a ladder.</li>
<li><strong>Climbers.</strong> Chain link gives toeholds. Smooth board or vinyl faces don't.</li>
<li><strong>Squeezers.</strong> Small dogs get through gaps between pickets, under gates, and beside gate posts. Board-on-board or no-gap vinyl panels close those off.</li>
<li><strong>Fence-line barkers.</strong> Dogs that react to every passing neighbor or dog usually settle down behind a solid privacy fence, because they can't see what sets them off.</li>
</ul>

<h2>On acreage</h2>
<p>Fencing a few acres in privacy wood gets expensive fast. Most acreage owners around Magnolia and Pinehurst fence a smaller dog yard near the house in wood, vinyl, or <a href="/materials/chain-link/">chain link</a>, then enclose the larger area with woven no-climb wire. The tight mesh keeps paws and heads from going through, and it's the same wire many owners already use for horses. See <a href="/materials/farm-ranch/">farm and ranch materials</a>.</p>
<p>Two things to plan for out here: low spots along a creek or drainage where the ground washes out under the wire, and keeping dogs and livestock apart if you have both. A separate dog yard solves both problems.</p>
<h2>On lake and golf lots</h2>
<p>Lots in Bentwater, April Sound, and other west Lake Conroe communities often limit the lake or fairway side to open ornamental fencing. Standard ornamental picket spacing will hold a big dog but can let a small one slip through. Some aluminum styles come with tighter-spaced pickets along the bottom, and a mesh liner is another option, but check with your architectural committee first, since both change the look.</p>
<h2>Gates are the weak point</h2>
<p>A fence is only as secure as its gates, and gates get left open by guests, kids, and service crews. For dog yards we recommend self-closing hinges and a self-latching latch, a gap at the bottom no bigger than the rest of the fence, and a lockable latch on any gate a lawn crew uses. If a gate has started to sag and leave a gap, our guide to <a href="/resources/fix-sagging-fence-gate/">fixing a sagging gate</a> can help.</p>
