<div class="callout76">
<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Get every bid in writing with the materials, post setting, gates, and tear-out listed line by line. Then compare the paperwork behind it: insurance certificate, warranty terms, who handles 811 and approvals, and when money changes hands.</p>
</div>
<h2>The quote itself</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Footage and height</strong>, measured on site rather than from an aerial photo, especially on irregular or wooded lots.</li>
<li><strong>Material grade.</strong> Cedar or pressure-treated pine, picket thickness, metal gauge or wall thickness, wire type for pasture fence.</li>
<li><strong>Gates</strong> listed separately, with width, framing, hinges, and latches. A 12- or 16-foot drive gate on acreage is a different item from a walk gate.</li>
<li><strong>Tear-out and haul-off</strong> of the old fence, and whether it's included or extra. On our replacements it's included unless you'd rather do it.</li>
<li><strong>Cleanup</strong>, including what happens to dirt from the post holes.</li>
</ul>
<h2>How it's built</h2>
<p>This is where bids differ most and say the least. Look for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Post depth and the amount of concrete per post, plus what changes at gate and corner posts. Ours start at 24 inches with 80 lb of concrete and go deeper in loose sand.</li>
<li>How rails attach to posts (brackets versus toe-nailing) and what fasteners are used.</li>
<li>Whether a rot board is part of a wood fence.</li>
<li>On acreage, how corners and braces are built, since they hold the tension on a wire fence.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Paperwork and protection</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Insurance certificate</strong> showing general liability, and whether the company carries workers' comp. See <a href="/faq/licensed-and-insured/">licensed and insured</a> for why Texas makes this the thing to check.</li>
<li><strong>811 locates</strong>: who places the request, and will they give you the ticket number.</li>
<li><strong>Permits and HOA approval</strong>: who prepares the drawing and specs, and that work won't start before written approval.</li>
<li><strong>Warranty in writing</strong>, with its length, start date, and exclusions. Compare ours on the <a href="/warranty/">warranty page</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Payment schedule</strong>: what's due at signing, what's due at completion, and whether the balance waits until after the walkthrough.</li>
<li><strong>Financing</strong>, if you need it, and whose program it is.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Extra checks for lake and acreage properties</h2>
<p>On a Bentwater, Walden, or Grand Harbor lot, ask how the contractor will handle the SJRA's 201-foot line and the architectural committee's rules for the water side. On five acres off FM 1774, ask whether they'll walk the full line with you, how they'll handle brush and creek crossings, and whether the gate posts are sized for a future opener.</p>
<p>Last, look at work they've finished nearby, in person if you can drive past it. A fence that's three or four years old shows more than a photo of one built last week. When you're ready to compare, <a href="/contact/">book a free estimate</a> and hold our written quote up against this list.</p>
