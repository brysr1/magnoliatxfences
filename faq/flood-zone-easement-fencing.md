<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> In many cases, yes, but the answer depends on three separate things: the FEMA flood zone, any easement on your plat, and, on a lake lot, the SJRA boundary. Each can change the permit, the fence style, or whether a fence belongs there at all.</p>
</div>
<h2>Check the map and the plat first</h2>
<p>Flood zones and easements get mixed up, but they come from different places. Your flood zone is on FEMA's flood maps, which you can look up by address at the <a href="https://msc.fema.gov/" rel="noopener">FEMA Map Service Center</a>. Easements are on your recorded plat and your survey, shown as strips along the back or side lot lines labeled for drainage or utilities.</p>
<p>Around Magnolia, both show up most in three places: acreage that backs to Mill Creek, Lake Creek, or Spring Creek; low draws on wooded acreage; and the engineered swales in newer subdivisions. Some communities make the map easy to find. The <a href="/neighborhoods/cimarron-country/">Cimarron Country</a> POA posts a FEMA floodplain map for the neighborhood, and its deed restrictions call for road culverts of at least 18 inches, so a fence or gate post can't crowd the ditch.</p>
<h2>Montgomery County's floodplain permit</h2>
<p>Outside city limits, the county runs the floodplain program. Its current <a href="https://cms1files.revize.com/montgomerycountytx/Development%20Regulations%20-%20Adopted%203-4-2025.pdf" rel="noopener">development regulations</a> define development as any man-made change to real estate and say all development in the floodplain must meet the county's floodplain rules and be permitted through its Floodplain Administration Department. The county's <a href="https://www-04.landandfarm.com/api/documents/3983707421/Montgomery_County_Floodplain_Management_Regulations_.pdf" rel="noopener">floodplain management regulations</a> exempt one fence by name: "A development permit will not be required for a three-strand barbed wire fence" (Article V, Section F). They also prohibit encroachments in the regulatory floodway unless an engineering analysis shows no rise in flood levels (Article V, Section E).</p>
<p>In practice, that means a three-strand barbed wire run across a creek bottom is the easy case, and a wood privacy fence or a no-climb horse fence inside the mapped floodplain is one we check with the county before building. A fence in the floodway itself is the hardest case. Inside Magnolia or Montgomery city limits, the city's rules apply as well; see <a href="/faq/fence-permits-magnolia-montgomery/">fence permits around Magnolia and Montgomery</a>.</p>
<h2>Lake Conroe lots</h2>
<p>On the west shore, the SJRA's 201-foot contour is the lake's normal pool and the edge of Authority Land, where a permanent structure needs an SJRA permit under its <a href="https://www.sjra.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Lake-Conroe-Rules-and-Regulations-2015.pdf" rel="noopener">Lake Conroe rules</a>. Above that, the <a href="https://www.sjra.net/?p=9666" rel="noopener">SJRA lists a flowage easement</a> up to 207 feet, the area it can raise the lake into during a flood. The record is 206.24 feet, set August 28, 2017, during Harvey. In the May 2024 storms the lake reached about 205 feet, <a href="https://communityimpact.com/houston/conroe-montgomery/government/2024/05/03/flood-watch-dam-releases-continue-in-montgomery-county-as-waterways-crest" rel="noopener">Community Impact reported</a>. A fence low on a <a href="/neighborhoods/bentwater/">Bentwater</a> or Walden lot can sit under water in a big event, so we plan the low end as open fencing or stop the run short of the slope.</p>
<h2>Easements: access and flow</h2>
<p>A drainage easement exists so water, and the crews who maintain the ditch, can get through. A utility easement holds lines that may need digging up. Deed restrictions or the easement holder can limit what goes there. If a fence must cross one, plan for a removable section or a gate wide enough for equipment.</p>
<p>Newer subdivisions around Magnolia are graded to move water along rear lot lines and between homes, and our <a href="/resources/new-construction-fence-magnolia-tx/">new construction guide</a> covers what that means for a first fence. The county holds developers to the same idea: when its regulations require a new subdivision to fence a shared line with existing homes, the fence must not block drainage from offsite properties (Section 3(C)(18)).</p>
<h2>Fences that let water pass</h2>
<ul>
<li>Ornamental aluminum or steel, which leaves most of the face open</li>
<li>Split rail or ranch rail, the classic choice along a creek bottom</li>
<li>Wire fencing with a water gap where it crosses a creek or ditch</li>
<li>Wood privacy raised a few inches off grade where it crosses a swale, so sheet flow isn't dammed against the boards</li>
</ul>
<p>Solid privacy panels catch debris and push water sideways into a neighbor's yard, which is why we steer away from them across a drainage path. Posts in low ground also need more care; holes there can fill with water as we dig, and our <a href="/resources/fence-posts-montgomery-county-soil/">soil guide</a> explains how we handle it. Our <a href="/materials/farm-ranch/">farm and ranch fencing</a> and <a href="/materials/ornamental-steel-aluminum/">ornamental metal</a> pages show the open styles in more detail, and the <a href="/pricing-guide/">pricing guide</a> lists typical per-foot ranges for each.</p>
