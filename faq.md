<div class="faq76">
<details><summary>Do I need a permit for a fence in unincorporated Montgomery County vs. the town of Montgomery?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>It depends on where the property sits. Much of the Magnolia and Lake Conroe area is unincorporated Montgomery County, where a typical residential fence usually doesn't need a building permit &mdash; but deed restrictions and HOA rules still apply. Properties inside the City of Montgomery limits can be subject to the city's own requirements. We check which rules apply to your address before we quote, and if a permit is needed, we'll tell you up front.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>What does HOA or ARC approval involve in communities like Bentwater or April Sound?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Most lake and golf communities ask for an application that shows where the fence will go, how tall it will be, what it's made of, and its color. Waterfront and fairway lots often have tighter limits so views stay open, which usually points toward ornamental aluminum or steel. We'll help put together the site sketch and material details, and we only start building once you have written approval.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>What's the cheapest fence option?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>For most yards, galvanized chain link is the lowest cost per foot, followed by pressure-treated pine. On acreage, barbed wire and field wire are the most economical for long runs. The cheapest fence isn't always the best value, though &mdash; we'll show you how options compare over the life of the fence, not just on installation day.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Do you handle 811 utility locates?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes, on every job. We request 811 locates before digging so gas, electric, water, and communication lines are marked. Private lines you've added yourself, like sprinkler or landscape lighting runs, aren't marked by 811, so let us know about those.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Do you build acreage and ranch fencing?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>We do. That includes property-line fencing, no-climb horse fence, field and woven wire, barbed wire, pipe and rail fencing, and wide drive and pasture gates. See our <a href="/acreage-ranch-fencing/">acreage &amp; ranch fencing</a> page for more.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Is financing available?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Financing options are available through the 76 Fence family for qualifying projects. Ask about it during your estimate and we'll walk you through what's offered and how it works.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>How long does a fence project take?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Most residential fences are installed in one to a few days once materials are on site. The bigger variables are usually before that: scheduling the estimate, HOA approval, and 811 locates. Large acreage jobs and custom gates take longer, and we'll give you a realistic timeline with your quote.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Should I repair my fence or replace it?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>If the posts are solid and the damage is limited to a few pickets, a section, or a gate, repair is usually the smart move. If posts are leaning or rotting along much of the line, patching tends to buy only a little time. We'll look at the whole fence and give you an honest recommendation either way.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Do you remove the old fence?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes. When we replace a fence, tear-out and haul-off of the old material are included in the quote unless you tell us you'd like to handle it yourself.</p></div></details>

See the full list of neighborhoods on our <a href="/service-areas/">service areas</a> page.</p></div></details>
