<section class="faq-group76">
<h2>Getting started</h2>
<div class="faq76">
<details><summary>What types of fences do you install?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Cedar and pressure-treated wood, vinyl, chain link, aluminum, steel and ornamental, composite, and farm and ranch fencing, plus gates, automatic gate openers, and pool enclosures. See our <a href="/materials/">materials</a> and <a href="/fence-styles/">fence styles</a> pages for each one.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Which areas do you serve?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Magnolia, Pinehurst, and the surrounding acreage communities, plus the town of Montgomery and the Lake Conroe area. See the full list of neighborhoods on our <a href="/service-areas/">service areas</a> page.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Do you install residential, commercial, and ranch fencing?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes to all three. See <a href="/residential-fencing/">residential</a>, <a href="/commercial-fencing/">commercial</a>, and <a href="/acreage-ranch-fencing/">acreage &amp; ranch fencing</a>.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Are estimates free?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes. <a href="/contact/">Book a free estimate</a> online or call (281) 292-1897. We can often give a ballpark by phone or from photos, but most final quotes come after we measure on site.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>What happens between the estimate and the finished fence?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Estimate, written quote, approval, then the paperwork: HOA approval if your neighborhood needs it, any permit, and Texas 811 locates. Then installation and a final walkthrough with you. Our <a href="/process/">process page</a> walks through each step.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>How long does a fence project take?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Most residential fences are installed in one to a few days once materials are on site. The bigger variables are usually before that: scheduling the estimate, HOA approval, and 811 locates. Large acreage jobs and custom gates take longer, and we'll give you a realistic timeline with your quote.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Do you set posts in concrete?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes, on every job. Posts go at least 24&Prime; deep with 80&nbsp;lb of concrete each, and deeper or wider where the ground is loose sand or holds water. Gate and corner posts get extra depth because they carry the most load. The right depth depends on fence height and what we find in the hole, not one number for the whole county.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Do you remove the old fence?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes. When we replace a fence, tear-out and haul-off of the old material are included in the quote unless you tell us you'd like to handle it yourself.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Do you handle 811 utility locates?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes, on every job. We request 811 locates before digging so gas, electric, water, and communication lines are marked. Private lines you've added yourself, like sprinkler or landscape lighting runs, aren't marked by 811, so let us know about those.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Do I need a property survey?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Not for every project, but a survey (or marked corner pins) is the clearest way to confirm where your property line runs before we build. It matters most on acreage, along shared lines with a neighbor, and anywhere a fence needs to stay out of an easement.</p></div></details>

<section class="faq-group76">
<h2>Permits, HOAs &amp; property lines</h2>
<div class="faq76">
<details><summary>Do I need a permit for a fence in unincorporated Montgomery County vs. the town of Montgomery?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>It depends on where the property sits. Much of the Magnolia and Lake Conroe area is unincorporated Montgomery County, where a typical residential fence usually doesn't need a building permit &mdash; but deed restrictions and HOA rules still apply. Properties inside the City of Montgomery limits can be subject to the city's own requirements. We check which rules apply to your address before we quote, and if a permit is needed, we'll tell you up front.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Does my HOA need to approve my fence?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>In most deed-restricted neighborhoods, yes. Many HOAs and architectural committees review height, style, material, stain color, and which side faces out. We help put together the drawing and material details your board asks for, and we build once you have written approval. See <a href="/hoa-fencing/">HOA fencing</a>.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>What does HOA or ARC approval involve in communities like Bentwater or April Sound?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Most lake and golf communities ask for an application that shows where the fence will go, how tall it will be, what it's made of, and its color. Waterfront and fairway lots often have tighter limits so views stay open, which usually points toward ornamental aluminum or steel. We'll help put together the site sketch and material details, and we only start building once you have written approval.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Who pays for a fence on the property line?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Texas has no general law that requires neighbors to split the cost of a standard residential fence 50/50, whatever you may have heard. Neighbors often agree to share the cost, and putting that agreement in writing helps. Your deed restrictions may also say who can build on the line and how. This isn&rsquo;t legal advice; for a dispute, talk with a real estate attorney.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>What if my lot has a drainage easement or is in a flood zone?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Extra rules can apply. A fence built across a drainage or utility easement can block water or access and may have to be removed at your expense, so check your plat or survey first. On low or flood-prone ground we also plan post depth and fence style so the fence doesn&rsquo;t act as a dam. We&rsquo;ll flag anything we see during the estimate.</p></div></details>
</div>

<section class="faq-group76">
<h2>Cost &amp; financing</h2>
<div class="faq76">
<details><summary>How much does a fence cost?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>It depends on material, height, length, gates, and the ground. Our <a href="/pricing-guide/">pricing guide</a> lists typical installed per-foot ranges for each material, but those are a ballpark. An exact price takes a site visit.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>What's the cheapest fence option?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>For most yards, galvanized chain link is the lowest cost per foot, followed by pressure-treated pine. On acreage, barbed wire and field wire are the most economical for long runs. The cheapest fence isn't always the best value, though &mdash; we'll show you how options compare over the life of the fence, not just on installation day.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Is financing available?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes, through Hearth. Hearth has options for credit scores of 550 and up, and qualified applicants with a score of 680 or higher may be eligible for 0% offers. Exact rates and terms depend on your application. See <a href="/financing/">financing</a>.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>When is the best time of year to install a fence?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>We install year-round. Spring and early summer are the busiest, so booking in fall or winter can mean a shorter wait. If your HOA needs to approve the fence, start that application early, since it can add a few weeks.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Which fence adds the most value to a home?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>It depends more on your street and your HOA than on any one material. In most subdivisions a well-built privacy fence that matches the neighborhood is what buyers expect. On lake and golf lots, open ornamental fencing that keeps the view tends to fit best. On acreage, sound perimeter and pasture fencing matters most.</p></div></details>
</div>

<section class="faq-group76">
<h2>Materials &amp; weather</h2>
<div class="faq76">
<details><summary>Which materials hold up best in our heat, humidity, and soil?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Aluminum, vinyl, and composite need the least upkeep. Cedar holds up better here than pressure-treated pine, which tends to twist over time. Whatever the material, how the posts are set matters as much as the boards. See <a href="/materials/">materials</a> for a side-by-side.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Is wood or vinyl better for this climate?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Both hold up well. Wood costs less up front and needs staining or sealing every few years. Vinyl costs more but doesn&rsquo;t need stain, paint, or rot repair. Your HOA&rsquo;s rules often settle the question. See <a href="/materials/wood-cedar/">wood &amp; cedar</a> and <a href="/materials/vinyl/">vinyl</a>.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>How long will a fence last?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>It depends on the material, the soil, and upkeep. Metal, vinyl, and composite often last 20 years or more with little maintenance. A well-built cedar fence that&rsquo;s stained and kept off the ground lasts much longer than a basic pine fence set in loose soil.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>What's a rot board, and do you use one?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>A rot board is a horizontal board along the bottom of a wood fence that takes the ground moisture instead of the pickets, and it can be replaced on its own. We put one on every wood fence we build unless you ask us not to.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Why do fence posts lean in our soil?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Much of Montgomery County has a layer of sand over clay. Sand digs easily but doesn&rsquo;t hold a post well on its own, and the clay underneath swells when wet and shrinks when dry. Shallow posts with too little concrete are what lean first. We set posts deeper and wider where the ground calls for it, especially at gates and corners.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>What fence holds up best in high wind and hurricane season?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Well-set aluminum or steel, and properly braced wood with enough post depth, tend to do best. Solid privacy panels catch the most wind, so post depth, spacing, and rail attachment matter more on those. Before storm season, check for loose boards, leaning posts, and gates that don&rsquo;t latch.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Will vinyl warp in Texas heat?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Rarely, with quality vinyl that&rsquo;s installed correctly. Post spacing and rail support matter more than most people expect, and dark colors absorb more heat than white or tan.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>What's the best fence for dogs?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Board-on-board wood privacy, solid vinyl privacy, and chain link with a buried bottom wire are common choices. Height, gaps, and digging are what to plan for. See <a href="/fence-styles/pet/">pet &amp; dog fences</a>.</p></div></details>
</div>

<section class="faq-group76">
<h2>Pools, gates, repairs &amp; warranty</h2>
<div class="faq76">
<details><summary>What are the pool fence requirements?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Pool barriers are commonly required to be at least 4 feet tall, with limits on gaps and climbable footholds, and gates that are self-closing and self-latching. Exact rules depend on your city or county and your HOA, so check with them for your property. See <a href="/fence-styles/pool-enclosure/">pool enclosures</a>.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Do you build walk gates, drive gates, and custom gates?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes. We build most walk gates on site, and fabricate ornate custom metal gates ahead of time. See <a href="/fence-styles/gates/">gates &amp; gate openers</a>.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Do you install automatic gate openers?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes. We source and install both solar and wired automatic operators, with keypads, remotes, and safety sensors.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Should I repair my fence or replace it?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>If the posts are solid and the damage is limited to a few pickets, a section, or a gate, repair is usually the smart move. If posts are leaning or rotting along much of the line, patching tends to buy only a little time. We'll look at the whole fence and give you an honest recommendation either way.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Do you stain or seal fences?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes. Staining or sealing a wood fence every few years helps it hold up to our sun and humidity. See <a href="/maintenance-repair/">maintenance &amp; repair</a>.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>What warranty do you offer?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Every installation is covered by an 18-month limited workmanship warranty, starting the day the project is completed. If a problem comes directly from improper installation by our crew in that window, we&rsquo;ll fix the affected area at no cost. It doesn&rsquo;t cover storms and other weather, normal wear, soil movement, impact damage, tree roots, or misuse, and material warranties come separately from the manufacturer. Full terms are on our <a href="/warranty/">warranty page</a>.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Are you insured?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Yes. We carry general liability and workers&rsquo; compensation insurance on every job.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>What if something goes wrong after installation?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Call or text us with a short description and a few photos, and we&rsquo;ll schedule an inspection to see whether it&rsquo;s covered by our workmanship warranty. The most common call is a gate that no longer latches cleanly as new posts settle. That&rsquo;s expected, and we&rsquo;ll come adjust it free of charge.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>What should I ask before hiring any fence contractor?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Ask how deep the posts go and how much concrete is in each, how rails are attached, whether they handle HOA paperwork and 811 locates, whether they carry liability and workers&rsquo; comp insurance, and what the warranty covers in writing. Get the materials spelled out on the quote.</p></div></details>
</div>
