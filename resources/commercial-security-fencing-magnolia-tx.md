<p>FM 1488 between Magnolia and the SH 249 tollway, and FM 1774 running north and south through town, have filled in with businesses that need a good fence: contractor and landscaping yards, boat and RV storage for the Lake Conroe crowd, small industrial lots, churches and schools, retail centers, and the amenity centers that come with each new subdivision. Each one needs something different. They share the same headaches, though: after-hours access, trucks coming and going all day, and a property that has to stay open while the fence goes in.</p>
<p>This guide is for owners and property managers planning a commercial fence. For a side-by-side of the materials, see our guide to <a href="/resources/security-fence-types/">security fence types</a>.</p>

<p>Most properties mix types. Ornamental steel where customers see it and chain link along the back line is a common way to keep the look up and the cost down.</p>

<h2>Plan gates around traffic, not just the opening</h2>
<p>On a commercial site, the gates get more wear than the rest of the fence combined. Size the vehicle opening for the longest rig that uses it, and leave room off the road so a truck isn't stopped in a lane of FM 1488 while the gate opens. A cantilever slide gate keeps the whole opening clear with no ground track for gravel and sand to jam.</p>
<p>Under the UL 325 standard, an operator on a gate the public uses, such as a storage facility or retail lot, falls in Class II; an industrial site closed to the public is Class III. Either way it needs monitored entrapment protection, controls set back from the gate, and a separate walk gate where people are on foot. Emergency access belongs in the plan for any gated site, so check with the fire marshal for your jurisdiction. Our <a href="/resources/automatic-gate-opener-acreage/">gate opener guide</a> covers the safety rules in more detail.</p>

<h2>Dumpster enclosures and screening</h2>
<p>Many commercial site plans require dumpsters, and sometimes mechanical equipment, to be screened from the street. An enclosure has to fit the container plus clearance, with double gates wide enough for the hauler's truck to service it without clipping a post, and posts set to take regular bumps. Ask your hauler for container and truck dimensions before the design is final. See <a href="/dumpster-enclosures/">dumpster enclosures</a> for materials and details.</p>

<h2>HOA pools and amenity centers</h2>
<p>Association pools fall under a specific state law. If your association runs the pool, Chapter 757 of the Texas Health and Safety Code covers it (the same chapter governs apartment complex pools). The enclosure must stand 48 inches or taller, every gate has to close and latch by itself, swing out away from the water, and take a lock. Chain link isn't allowed for enclosures built after January 1, 1994. There are also limits on gaps and latch height. Boards and managers can read the full text on the <a href="https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/HS/htm/HS.757.htm" rel="noopener">Texas statutes site</a>; this is general information, not legal advice, so check specifics with the association's attorney.</p>

<h2>Permits, site plans, and which rules apply</h2>
<p>Where the property sits decides the rules. Fence is one of the permit types the City of Magnolia issues, and commercial sites inside city limits may also have screening requirements tied to their site plan, so start with the city's permit department. A lot of FM 1488 and FM 1774 frontage lies outside city limits in unincorporated Montgomery County, where the county's floodplain rules can still apply to work in a mapped floodplain. Inside the City of Montgomery, the city asks anyone unsure about a permit to call 936-597-3304. Before quoting, we check the jurisdiction for your address; our <a href="/faq/fence-permits-magnolia-montgomery/">permit guide</a> has the details.</p>

<h2>Working around your business, and the paperwork</h2>
<p>Most businesses can't close for a fence install. We schedule around business hours, work in sections so an entrance stays usable, and plan how the site stays secure overnight while the new gate isn't hung yet. Tell us when deliveries and peak traffic happen and who our contact on site will be.</p>
<p>On the paperwork side, we carry general liability and workers' compensation insurance, and certificates are available on request, so send your landlord's or manager's requirements early. You'll get a written quote your team can approve. We request Texas 811 locates before digging, but commercial sites often have private lines for parking-lot lights, signs, and irrigation that 811 won't mark, so a site plan or as-built drawing helps. Our page on <a href="/faq/licensed-and-insured/">licensing and insurance</a> explains what to ask any contractor.</p>
