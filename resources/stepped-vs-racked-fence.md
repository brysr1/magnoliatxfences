<p>The stepped vs. racked fence question comes up on a lot of lots around Magnolia and Pinehurst. The yard looks flat until you stretch a string line across it, then the back corner turns out to sit two feet lower than the patio. Rear lot lines in newer subdivisions are graded to carry water, older acreage drops toward creek bottoms, and lake lots out toward Montgomery slope down to Lake Conroe.</p>
<p>A fence can handle that grade two ways. A <strong>stepped</strong> fence keeps each section level and drops it down at every post, like stairs. A <strong>racked</strong> fence tilts each section so the rails run parallel to the ground and the top line follows the slope. Here's how they compare.</p>

<h2>How each one looks</h2>
<p>A racked fence reads as one smooth line that rolls with the yard. On a gentle, steady grade it looks the most natural, and the pickets stay plumb while the rails angle.</p>
<p>A stepped fence has a stair-step top. On a steep or uneven slope that can look cleaner than a fence that bends and twists, and it suits formal styles where level rails matter. On a long, gentle grade, though, steps can look choppy.</p>

<h2>The gap underneath</h2>
<p>This is the biggest practical difference. A racked section follows the ground, so the gap under the bottom stays about the same the whole way down.</p>
<p>A stepped section stays level, so a triangle-shaped gap opens at the low end of each step. On a steep slope those triangles get big enough for a small dog to slip through or for an armadillo to wander in. We can close them with a rot board cut to the grade, a bit of extra picket length, or by shortening the panel width, but it's work to plan for up front.</p>
<p>If the fence is part of a pool barrier, those gaps matter more, since the barrier has to stop a child from getting under it. Tell us up front if a pool is part of the plan so we lay out the steps with that in mind.</p>

<h2>Which materials can do which</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Wood privacy.</strong> Built on site, so it can be racked or stepped. Racking is our usual choice on gentle grades.</li>
<li><strong>Ornamental aluminum and steel.</strong> Many panels are made to rack, within limits the manufacturer sets for each style. Past that limit, the panels have to step. Our <a href="/materials/aluminum/">aluminum fencing</a> page explains why it works well on lots sloping toward the water.</li>
<li><strong>Vinyl.</strong> Most privacy panels are routed and don't rack much, so vinyl on a slope is usually stepped.</li>
<li><strong>Chain link and ranch wire.</strong> The fabric follows the ground on its own, so these are racked by nature.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Cost</h2>
<p>Neither is automatically cheaper. On a wood fence, racking takes more careful layout and cutting, while stepping on a steep grade may need taller posts and extra boards to fill the gaps. With pre-made panels, staying within the rack limit is usually the simplest install, and steep stretches may need more posts or special panels. Either way, slope gets priced from what we see on site, not from a flat per-foot number.</p>

<h2>Drainage and the low spots</h2>
<p>Slopes here are often there to move water. A swale along the back of the lot or the low end of a lake lot carries runoff after a heavy rain, and a fence that dams it can push water toward your house or your neighbor's. Racked fences make it easier to hold a steady, small gap above grade. Where a fence crosses a swale, we often lift it a few inches or switch to an open style. Our page on <a href="/faq/flood-zone-easement-fencing/">fencing in flood zones and easements</a> explains the rules and the lake levels on the west side of Lake Conroe.</p>

<h2>Posts on a slope</h2>
<p>Whichever you pick, the posts do the real work. Loose sand doesn't grip a post sideways, and on a slope the downhill side of each hole has even less soil behind it. Every post goes in concrete, with at least 24 inches and 80 pounds of concrete on a wood fence, and the posts at the bottom of the grade, at corners, and at gates get deeper, wider piers belled at the base. If water collects in a hole at the low end, we pump or bail it out before the pour. Our guide to <a href="/resources/fence-posts-montgomery-county-soil/">setting posts in Montgomery County soil</a> goes into detail.</p>

<h2>Which one we'd pick</h2>
<p>For most sloped yards around Magnolia with a gentle, steady grade, a racked fence looks better and keeps pets in with less fuss. Choose stepped when the ground drops sharply, when you want vinyl, or when your HOA or the style calls for level rails. Many yards end up with a little of both.</p>
