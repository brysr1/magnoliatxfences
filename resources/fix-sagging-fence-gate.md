<p>Gates take more abuse than any other part of a fence. They get opened dozens of times a week, pushed with a mower or a trash can, and left swinging in the wind. All of that weight hangs off one post and two or three hinges, so when something gives, the gate shows it first.</p>
<p>On Magnolia-area properties the gates also tend to run bigger than on a typical city lot. Ten- and twelve-foot double drive gates for trailers, wide side gates for riding mowers, and long pipe gates at acreage entrances are all common. The bigger the gate, the more it pays to diagnose the sag correctly before you start fixing.</p>

<h2>Three tests that tell you what moved</h2>
<p>You can sort out most sagging gates in a few minutes with a level, a tape measure, and a helper.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Lift the latch end.</strong> Close the gate and lift the free end a couple of inches. If the latch lines up and the gate swings freely while lifted, the hinges or frame are the issue. If lifting barely helps, look at the post.</li>
<li><strong>Put a level on the hinge post.</strong> Check it on two faces. A post that leans toward the opening drags the whole gate down with it, and no hinge will fix that.</li>
<li><strong>Measure corner to corner.</strong> On a rectangular gate, both diagonals should be the same length. If one is noticeably longer, the frame has racked into a parallelogram and the joints have loosened.</li>
</ol>

<h2>When the hinges are the weak point</h2>
<p>Look for screws that have backed out, hinge leaves that have bent, and screw holes that have stripped out in the wood. Hardware sold for light walk gates is often undersized for a six-foot privacy gate with full boards, especially a board-on-board gate that carries pickets on both sides.</p>
<p>Replace short screws with longer exterior-rated structural screws or through-bolts that reach solid wood. If the holes are stripped, move the hinge slightly or fill the old holes with glued hardwood dowels and redrill. On a heavy gate, upgrade to heavy-duty strap hinges and add a third hinge in the middle.</p>

<h2>When the frame has racked</h2>
<p>A wood gate frame stays square because of a diagonal brace. The brace should run from the bottom corner on the hinge side up to the top corner on the latch side, so it pushes the free end up. A brace installed the other way does very little, and we see that more often than you'd think.</p>
<p>For a gate that is already out of square, an anti-sag kit is a good fix. It's a steel cable with a turnbuckle that runs the opposite way from the brace, from the top hinge corner down to the bottom latch corner. Tighten it a little at a time until the diagonals match and the latch lines up. On vinyl gates, the frame depends on an internal metal insert, and if that insert was left out or has slipped, the gate may need to be rebuilt.</p>

<h2>When the post is the problem</h2>
<p>If the hinge post leans, everything else is a patch. Gate posts carry far more leverage than line posts because the gate's weight sits out at the end of an arm. In the sandy topsoil around Magnolia and Pinehurst, a gate post set at line-post depth often starts to tip within a few years.</p>
<p>The real repair is to pull the post and set a new one deeper and wider in concrete, or to replace a rotted wood post with steel. That's why we set gate posts deeper than line posts on every fence we build. Our guide to <a href="/resources/fix-leaning-fence-post-sandy-soil/">fixing a leaning post</a> explains how to tell rot from a shallow footing.</p>

<h2>Drive gates and acreage entrance gates</h2>
<p>Double drive gates need a center stop. A drop rod that seats into a sleeve in the driveway, or a stop set in the ground, gives both leaves something to close against. Without it, wind and traffic push the leaves past each other until the hinges loosen. Check that the sleeve hasn't filled with sand or gravel, which keeps the rod from seating.</p>
<p>Long pipe gates at a pasture or ranch entrance often sag because the hinge post was never braced. On a wire fence, the gate post should be part of a brace assembly just like a corner. A gate that has to be lifted and dragged every morning is a good candidate for a rebuild, and if you're tired of getting out of the truck, an <a href="/resources/automatic-gate-opener-acreage/">automatic gate opener</a> needs a solid post and a square gate anyway.</p>

<h2>Building a gate that stays square</h2>
<p>When we build a new gate or rebuild an old one, we size it for what goes through it and hang it on hardware rated for its weight. A wood gate is built around a properly oriented brace or a welded steel frame, and a vinyl gate around a metal insert. Gates that see daily use and wide drive gates are worth building on steel. You can see the options on our <a href="/fence-styles/gates/">gates and gate openers</a> page.</p>
<p>A little upkeep helps too. Each fall, tighten hinge screws and latch hardware, lube hinges on metal gates, and keep soil and mulch from building up under the swing path. If a new gate stops latching cleanly after the posts settle, that's normal, and we'll come back and adjust it free of charge.</p>
