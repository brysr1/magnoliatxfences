<p>On acreage around Magnolia, the drive gate is usually a long way from the house and sometimes a long way from power. You open it in the rain, in the dark, with a trailer behind you, and with the dogs trying to slip out. An automatic opener solves all of that, as long as the gate, posts, power, and safety equipment are planned together.</p>
<p>Here's what goes into choosing one for a rural entrance, and the safety rules that apply to any powered gate.</p>

<h2>Start with the gate and posts you already have</h2>
<p>An operator moves whatever hangs on the posts, so the gate has to be built for it. A ranch pipe gate that drags, flexes, or has dropped on its hinges will wear out a motor quickly. The UL 325 standard for gate operators says automatic gates and the fence next to them should be built to ASTM F2200, the industry standard for automated vehicle gate construction.</p>
<p>Posts matter just as much. Sandy topsoil over clay lets an undersized post lean, and a gate that's out of plumb binds at the latch and strains the operator. We can often add an opener to an existing gate if it's sturdy enough; if not, the posts or the gate get replaced first. See our <a href="/fence-styles/gates/">gates page</a> for the drive gates we build.</p>

<h2>Swing or slide on a country driveway</h2>
<p>Swing gates are the usual choice on acreage. They need a clear arc to open into, so look for a driveway that rises toward the house, a culvert headwall, or a ditch just inside the gate. On a slope, a gate swinging uphill can hit the ground before it's fully open. Splitting a wide opening into two leaves keeps each one lighter and easier on its operator.</p>
<p>Slide gates need room beside the opening for the gate to roll into, a bit more than the width of the opening itself, along a fairly level path. They don't need an arc and handle wind better than a big solid swing leaf, so they suit narrow entrances close to the road. Cantilever slide gates ride above the ground on rollers, which keeps sand and grass from fouling a track.</p>

<p>Solar is a common pick on acreage because running power a few hundred feet down a driveway gets expensive. Size the panel and battery for how many times a day the gate runs, and keep the panel clear of the tree line, which can take some planning on wooded lots. Wired operators make sense for heavy gates or heavy use. Under Texas licensing rules, the line-voltage feed to an operator is generally work for a licensed electrician, while low-voltage control wiring may be exempt, so ask your electrician what your setup needs. We coordinate with them when a wired operator is the plan.</p>

<h2>Safety: UL 325 in plain terms</h2>
<p>An automatic gate is heavy, and it can trap a person or an animal. UL 325 is the safety standard quality operators are built and listed to, and building codes reference it. The points that matter most at a home entrance:</p>
<ul>
<li>A single-family home falls under Class I, the residential usage class.</li>
<li>Swing and slide gates need two independent means of entrapment protection in each direction of travel, for example a photo eye plus a sensing edge. Any device mounted outside the operator has to be monitored, and newer operators, from 2016 on, refuse to run without one hooked up.</li>
<li>Controls have to be at least 6 feet from the gate, so nobody can reach through it to push a button.</li>
<li>A vehicle gate isn't a walk gate. Where people are likely to be on foot, a separate pedestrian gate is required.</li>
<li>Warning signs go on both sides of the gate.</li>
<li>Slide gates must be screened so a 2&frac14;-inch ball can't pass through, from the bottom up to 6 feet, which keeps hands and paws out of the moving gate.</li>
</ul>
<p>UL 325 isn't retroactive, and the gate industry's trade association recommends replacing operators made before 2000. If kids, horses, or dogs use the driveway, place photo eyes where they'll see what actually crosses the gate's path.</p>

<h2>Keypads, remotes, and room to pull off the road</h2>
<p>Most acreage owners carry remotes in their vehicles and use a keypad for deliveries, feed drops, and guests, with a code they can change. Mount the keypad on a pedestal the driver can reach from the window. More important, put the gate deep enough into the property that your rig, trailer included, is off FM 1774 or the county road entirely while you wait. Sitting with a trailer hanging into the lane is how wrecks happen. A vehicle sensor on the inside lets you drive out without reaching for a remote, and phone-app or call-box controls are options if cell signal at the gate is good.</p>

<h2>Storms and power outages</h2>
<p>Long outages after a hurricane are part of living here. Hurricane Beryl in July 2024 cut power to roughly 2.7 million people across Southeast Texas, according to the National Hurricane Center. Solar operators keep running on their batteries, and wired operators need a backup battery to do the same. Every operator should have a manual release; know where the key is and try it once before you need it. After a storm, check that the photo eyes are lined up and nothing has fallen into the gate's path before you run it.</p>
