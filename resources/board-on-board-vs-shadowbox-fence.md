<p>When someone in Magnolia or Pinehurst wants a wood privacy fence that's a step up from plain side-by-side pickets, the choice usually comes down to board on board vs. shadowbox fence. Both look more finished. Both hide the gaps that open up as pickets dry and shrink in our summer heat. They're built differently, though, and that difference shows up in privacy, wind, cost, and which side of the fence your neighbor ends up looking at.</p>
<p>Here's how the two styles compare, dimension by dimension.</p>

<h2>How each one is built</h2>
<p><strong>Board-on-board</strong> puts all the pickets on one side of the rails in two layers. The first layer goes up with gaps, and the second layer covers those gaps with an overlap on each edge. From the front it reads as a solid wall, and it stays that way even after the boards shrink.</p>
<p><strong>Shadowbox</strong> alternates pickets on each side of the rails: one board on the front, the next on the back, with the gaps narrower than a picket. Looking straight at it, you see a solid face. From an angle, you can see partway through. Our <a href="/fence-styles/privacy/">privacy fence page</a> shows how these fit alongside the other styles we build.</p>

<h2>Privacy</h2>
<p>Board-on-board is the clear winner. Nothing gets through it from any angle, which matters next to a two-story house, along a busy road, or around a hot tub.</p>
<p>Shadowbox gives most of the privacy, most of the time. Someone walking past at an angle can catch glimpses through the gaps. For a back property line behind the pines, that's usually fine. For a patio that sits ten feet from a neighbor's kitchen window, it may not be.</p>

<h2>Wind and post load</h2>
<p>A solid board-on-board fence acts like a sail in a strong gust, and all that push goes into the posts. Shadowbox lets air move through the staggered gaps, so the fence takes less load in a storm. We cover that trade-off in more detail in our guide to the <a href="/faq/best-fence-for-wind/">best fence for wind</a>.</p>
<p>Either way, the posts decide whether the fence stays straight. Most lots around Magnolia and Pinehurst have sandy loam on top, and loose sand doesn't grip a post sideways. Every post we set goes in concrete, 24 inches deep at minimum with 80 lb of concrete on a wood fence, and corners and gates get deeper, wider footings, belled at the base. A solid board-on-board run is where we're least willing to cut that short. Our article on <a href="/resources/fence-posts-montgomery-county-soil/">setting posts in Montgomery County soil</a> explains why.</p>

<h2>Looks from both sides</h2>
<p>Board-on-board has a good side and a back side. You choose which way the pickets face, and whoever is on the other side sees posts and rails. Shadowbox looks the same from both sides, so nobody gets the back of the fence.</p>
<p>That can matter in a subdivision. Some deed restrictions call for the finished side to face the street or common areas, and a shared fence is easier to agree on when both neighbors get the same view. Check your guidelines before you pick a style.</p>

<h2>Cost</h2>
<p>Both styles use more pickets than side-by-side, and that extra lumber and labor is most of the price difference. How much more depends on the picket width, the overlap, and the gap spacing. On a typical backyard the two styles usually land close to each other. Height, gates, cedar vs. pine, and any tear-out of an old fence move the final number more than the style does.</p>

<h2>Staining and upkeep</h2>
<p>Shadowbox has two exposed faces, so you're staining both sides of every picket if you want it to look even. Board-on-board has one main face to coat, but the overlapping edges take a sprayer and some patience to cover well.</p>
<p>Both styles leave a little more wood touching wood than side-by-side, so keep pine needles and leaves cleared from the bottom rail and rot board. A fence that dries out between rains lasts longer either way.</p>

<h2>The practical takeaway</h2>
<p>If full privacy is the reason you're building the fence, go board-on-board and make sure the posts are set for the wind load. If you're sharing the line with a neighbor, want some breeze through the yard, or your HOA cares how the fence looks from the other side, shadowbox is usually the better fit.</p>
