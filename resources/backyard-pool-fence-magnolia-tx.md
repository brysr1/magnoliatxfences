<p>A backyard pool fence is one of those projects that goes smoother when it's planned early. Around Magnolia and Pinehurst, a lot of pools go in on subdivision lots where the yard already has a wood privacy fence, and the pool fence gets treated as an afterthought. Then the deck is poured, the equipment pad is in the wrong spot, and the gate ends up where nobody wants to walk.</p>
<p>If you're planning a pool for next summer, now is a good time to plan the fence with it. Here's what we work through with homeowners before anything gets dug.</p>

<h2>Decide what the barrier actually is</h2>
<p>The first question is simple: what keeps a child out of the pool when no one is watching? There are two common setups.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>A separate pool enclosure:</strong> a fence around the pool and deck only, usually ornamental aluminum so you can still see the pool from the house. The pool stays closed off even if someone leaves a side yard gate open.</li>
<li><strong>The yard fence as the barrier:</strong> the perimeter fence does the job, which means every gate in it has to close and latch on its own. It costs less, but one propped-open side gate during a lawn service visit defeats the whole thing.</li>
</ul>
<p>Most codes treat the house wall as part of the barrier too, so doors that open toward the pool matter. If you have young kids or grandkids visiting, a separate enclosure is the safer layout. Our <a href="/fence-styles/pool-enclosure/">pool enclosure fence</a> page covers the heights and spacing most codes call for.</p>

<h2>Get the gates right</h2>
<p>The gate is where most pool fences fail, and it's rarely on day one. A pool gate should swing away from the pool, close on its own from any open position, and latch without anyone touching it. That only works if the gate stays square and the post stays plumb.</p>
<p>That's where local ground comes in. Most lots around Magnolia and Pinehurst have sandy loam on top of a clay subsoil, and loose sand doesn't grip a post sideways. A gate post that leans even slightly lets the gate drop, and a dropped gate stops short of the latch. We set every post in concrete, and gate posts go deeper and wider, with the pier belled at the base so it can't rock. Our guide to <a href="/resources/fence-posts-montgomery-county-soil/">setting fence posts in Montgomery County soil</a> explains why gates and corners lean first.</p>
<p>A few gate details we plan for on every pool fence:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Self-closing hinges with adjustable tension,</strong> so the gate can be tuned as it settles in.</li>
<li><strong>A latch high enough or on the pool side,</strong> so a small child reaching over or through can't work it.</li>
<li><strong>One walk gate if you can,</strong> placed on the path people already use from the back door to the pool.</li>
<li><strong>A separate service gate</strong> near the equipment pad, kept latched, so the pool tech doesn't need to cross the deck.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Remove the footholds</h2>
<p>A pool fence can meet every measurement and still be easy to climb. Walk the outside of the planned fence line and look for anything a child could stand on:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Horizontal rails on the outside:</strong> on a wood fence, the rails go on the pool side so the outside face is flat.</li>
<li><strong>The AC unit, pool equipment, or a planter</strong> set against the fence.</li>
<li><strong>Patio furniture and grills</strong> that tend to migrate toward the fence line.</li>
<li><strong>A low wall or raised bed</strong> that effectively shortens the fence.</li>
</ul>
<p>Plan the equipment pad and landscaping with the fence in mind. Moving a planter is free. Moving a fence after the deck is poured is not.</p>

<h2>Pick a material that likes pool water</h2>
<p>Splash, pool chemicals, and Texas humidity are hard on fencing. Aluminum won't rust, which is why it's the most common pool fence material we install. Powder-coated steel is stronger but needs scratches touched up. Vinyl or wood privacy works when you want the pool screened from neighbors, as long as the gates and climbable spots are handled the same way.</p>

<h2>Check the rules before you build</h2>
<p>Inside Magnolia or Montgomery city limits, fences need a city permit, and the city's code decides the pool barrier details. In unincorporated Montgomery County, your HOA or POA, your pool builder, and sometimes your insurer will still expect a code-style barrier. Our <a href="/faq/fence-permits-magnolia-montgomery/">permit guide</a> covers where to check.</p>
