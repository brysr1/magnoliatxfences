<p>If you live on the wooded side of Magnolia or out toward Pinehurst, you've probably watched deer walk through the yard like they own it. A deer fence is the one fix that works long term, but only if it's built for how deer actually move. A standard 4-foot wire fence or a short picket fence slows them down for about a season, then they learn to hop it.</p>
<p>Here's what we look at when someone around Magnolia asks us to keep deer out of a garden, an orchard, or a whole backyard.</p>

<h2>How tall a deer fence needs to be</h2>
<p>A white-tailed deer can clear a fence well over six feet when it's motivated. Wildlife agencies generally point to about eight feet for a vertical fence meant to keep deer out, and the <a href="https://ncwildlife.org/wildlife-habitat/land-management/fencing-exclude-deer" rel="noopener">North Carolina Wildlife Resources Commission's fencing guide</a> walks through fence designs built to exclude deer. Eight feet is the number we plan around for a true deer fence on open ground.</p>
<p>Height isn't the only factor, though. Deer are often reluctant to jump something they can't see over, because they can't see where they'll land. A solid 6-foot privacy fence around a small backyard can turn deer away more often than a see-through wire fence of the same height. It's not a guarantee, but it's why a privacy fence on a subdivision lot often ends the problem without going to eight feet.</p>

<h2>Wire or privacy: which deer fence fits your property</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Tall woven wire on acreage.</strong> For a garden plot, an orchard, or a few acres of pasture, 8-foot woven wire on tall posts is the practical choice. It nearly disappears from a distance, costs far less per foot than a solid fence, and lasts when the corners are braced right. Our <a href="/materials/farm-ranch/">farm and ranch materials</a> page covers the wire types and how they hold up in our humidity.</li>
<li><strong>Privacy fence on a subdivision lot.</strong> On a typical lot, a solid wood or composite privacy fence keeps deer out of the backyard and does everything else you want a fence to do. Many neighborhoods cap rear fences at six feet, so this is usually the deer fence you're allowed to build.</li>
<li><strong>A garden enclosure inside the yard.</strong> If deer only care about the vegetable beds or the fruit trees, fencing just that area to full height costs much less than wrapping the whole property.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Check the height limit before you plan eight feet</h2>
<p>An 8-foot fence isn't allowed everywhere. Inside the City of Magnolia or the City of Montgomery, height is set by city code, and deed restrictions in many subdivisions set their own limits. Out on unincorporated acreage there's usually no county permit for an ordinary fence, but a POA may still have rules. Our <a href="/faq/fence-permits-magnolia-montgomery/">fence permit FAQ</a> explains who governs which lots, and we confirm the limit for your address before we quote.</p>

<h2>Where deer fences usually fail</h2>
<h3>Gaps at the bottom</h3>
<p>Deer will often slip under a fence before they'll try to jump it. A gap where the ground dips, a washout under the wire after a heavy rain, or a stretch that rides up over a root is often all it takes. We keep the bottom tight to grade and check the low spots, especially where water runs.</p>
<h3>Gates left open or sagging</h3>
<p>The best deer fence in the county won't help if the gate stands open. On a garden or pasture fence, a gate that swings shut and latches on its own is worth the cost. A gate that sags leaves a wedge-shaped gap deer will find.</p>
<h3>Weak spots along the line</h3>
<p>A fallen pine limb that pins the wire down for a week can become the new crossing. On wooded acreage, walk the line after every storm.</p>

<h2>Posts for a taller fence in sandy ground</h2>
<p>An 8-foot fence catches more wind than a 4-foot one, and that load lands on the posts. Most Magnolia and Pinehurst lots have loose, sandy soil on top that doesn't grip a post sideways, so corners and gate posts lean first. Every post goes in concrete, never gravel alone. Twenty-four inches and 80 pounds of concrete is the floor for a wood fence, and a taller deer fence calls for deeper, wider holes, with corners and gates on piers belled at the base. If water seeps into a hole, we pump or bail it out before the pour. Our guide to <a href="/resources/fence-posts-montgomery-county-soil/">setting posts in Montgomery County soil</a> goes into the details.</p>

<h2>Getting a deer fence that lasts</h2>
<p>A good deer fence starts with how deer are getting in today: where the trails cross your line, what they're eating, and whether the problem is the whole yard or one garden. From there it's a question of height, style, and posts that stay put.</p>
