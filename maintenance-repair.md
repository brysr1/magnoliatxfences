<h2>Why fences fail out here</h2>
<p>Most calls for fence repair in Magnolia trace back to the same few causes. Montgomery County is hard on fences. Clay soil swells when it's wet and shrinks in a dry summer, which slowly pushes posts out of plumb. Long stretches of humidity rot pickets and rails from the bottom up, especially where sprinklers hit the same boards every morning. And a single tropical storm or a falling pine limb can take out a whole section overnight.</p>
<p>When you call us for a repair, we look at the whole fence &mdash; not just the broken part. If the rest of the fence is solid, we'll repair the damaged section to match. If the posts are failing along the entire line, we'll tell you plainly that a patch won't last and show you what a replacement would cost instead.</p>

<h2>Common repairs, and how we fix them</h2>
<h3>Leaning posts</h3>
<p>A leaning post tells you what went wrong underground. Usually it's rot at the ground line, a hole that was too shallow, sand washed out by a downspout, or roots pushing from below. A 2x4 brace can hold a post up for a week or two, but it isn't a repair. We pull the post, dig deeper and wider, and set it again in fresh concrete, or replace it if it's rotted. Every wood post we set goes at least 24 inches deep with 80 pounds of concrete, with more at gates and corners. Our guide to <a href="/resources/fix-leaning-fence-post-sandy-soil/">fixing a leaning post in sandy soil</a> explains how to read the lean.</p>
<h3>Storm damage</h3>
<p>After a storm, secure the opening first if you have pets or a pool, and take photos before anything gets cleaned up. We'll walk the damage, rebuild the sections that went down, and give you a written quote that lists the footage, posts, material, gates, and tear-out so your adjuster can match it to what they saw. We also note any wear that was there before the storm. Our guide to <a href="/resources/fence-storm-damage-insurance-claim/">storm damage and insurance claims</a> covers the first few days.</p>
<h3>Sagging gates</h3>
<p>A gate that drags is almost always the hinges, the frame, or the post. Loose hinges get longer structural screws or through-bolts, or heavier strap hinges. A frame that has racked out of square gets a cable-and-turnbuckle anti-sag kit or a rebuild on a welded steel frame. A leaning hinge post has to be reset deeper or replaced with steel. Double drive gates also need a center stop. See <a href="/resources/fix-sagging-fence-gate/">how to fix a sagging gate</a>.</p>
<h3>Rotted pickets</h3>
<p>Pickets and rails rot from the bottom up where sprinklers, mulch, or leaves keep them wet. We replace the soft pickets, rails, and rot board and match the existing wood as closely as we can. New boards will look brighter than a fence that has weathered for years. A stain helps the repair blend in, though it rarely disappears completely.</p>

<h2>Repair or replace?</h2>
<p>We start with the posts, not the pickets. One or two soft or leaning posts are a repair. Soft posts all down the line mean the fence is near the end, even if the boards still look fine.</p>
<p>Repair usually makes sense when the damage is in one area, most posts are solid and plumb, or the gate is the only problem. Replacement makes more sense once a third or more of the fence needs new posts, rails, or pickets, or when the same fence keeps needing repairs. At that point the two prices get close. A wood fence here often lasts about 10 to 20 years, so age matters too.</p>
<p>If it's close, we price both and let you choose. Our article on <a href="/resources/repair-or-replace-fence/">repairing or replacing a fence</a> goes into more detail.</p>

<h2>HOA approval for repairs</h2>
<p>In many deed-restricted neighborhoods, replacing a fence, extending it, adding a gate, or restaining it a new color counts as a modification that needs approval. Check your guidelines or ask the management company before work starts.</p>
<p>When approval is needed, we put together the sketch, material, height, and stain color your HOA or architectural committee asks for, and we don't start until you have approval in hand. A repaired section that faces the street matters more to a strict HOA, so we match boards and stain as closely as we can. Our <a href="/resources/hoa-fence-approval-magnolia-tx/">HOA approval guide</a> walks through the process.</p>
