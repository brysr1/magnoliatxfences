<p><strong>Look.</strong> An aluminum fence looks like black wrought iron from the street: slim vertical pickets, clean rails, and options like spear tops, finials, and decorative rings. It defines a yard without blocking light or views, which is why it's the go-to fence on golf-course and lakefront lots and around pools.</p>

<p><strong>Upkeep.</strong> Aluminum doesn't rust, which makes it a strong pick near the water and around pool chemicals. A scratch in the finish won't turn into a rust streak. It can pit or chalk where the finish is damaged and left exposed for a long time, so a yearly rinse and a look at the bottom of the posts keeps it in good shape. Self-closing pool gates benefit from a yearly check of hinges and latches.</p>

<p><strong>Cost.</strong> On our <a href="/pricing-guide/">pricing guide</a>, aluminum typically runs $40&ndash;$100 per linear foot installed. Taller sections and decorative gates add to the price. Need extra strength for a drive gate? See <a href="/materials/ornamental-steel-aluminum/">ornamental steel</a>.</p>

<h2>Where aluminum fits best</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Pool enclosures.</strong> The fence sits in splash range and sees chlorinated water every day. Aluminum handles it without rusting. See <a href="/fence-styles/pool-enclosure/">pool enclosures</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Lakefront lots.</strong> On the west side of Lake Conroe, fences live in damp air, sprinkler overspray, and splash.</li>
<li><strong>Damp, shady yards.</strong> Where the ground stays wet under the trees, aluminum is the low-worry choice.</li>
<li><strong>Sloped yards.</strong> Light panels are easy to handle and easy to rack along a slope toward the water.</li>
<li><strong>Mixed runs.</strong> Many owners use aluminum around the pool and yard, and steel for the drive gate.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>

<div class="inner76">
<h2>Acreage or subdivision lot?</h2>
<div class="cards76">
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On acreage</h3><p>On acreage, aluminum is usually used close to the house: the pool, the yard, and the front of the property. Running it along an entire multi-acre boundary is rarely cost-effective; ranch fencing usually takes over from there.</p></div></div>
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On subdivision lots</h3><p>On subdivision lots, aluminum fits front yards, side yards, and pool enclosures, and it's a common choice on golf-course and lakefront lots where guidelines call for open fencing. If your property has HOA or deed restrictions, check with your board on height and style.</p></div></div>
</div>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section76">
<div class="prose76">
<h2>Local rules</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>City of Magnolia.</strong> Inside city limits, a fence needs a city permit: $65 plus a $50 administrative fee on the November 2024 schedule.</li>
<li><strong>City of Montgomery.</strong> Fences are on the city's list of projects that need a permit.</li>
<li><strong>Unincorporated Montgomery County.</strong> A typical residential fence usually doesn't need a county permit, but HOA or POA approval may still apply.</li>
<li><strong>Lake and golf communities.</strong> Architectural committees in Bentwater, Walden, April Sound, and Grand Harbor review fence and pool barrier plans.</li>
<li><strong>Lake Conroe.</strong> On west-shore lots, the back of the lot can run into land the San Jacinto River Authority controls. We keep the fence off it.</li>
</ul>
<p>See <a href="/service-areas/magnolia-tx/">fencing in Magnolia</a> and our <a href="/faq/fence-permits-magnolia-montgomery/">permit guide</a>.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section76-alt full-bleed76">
<div class="inner76">
<h2>What affects the cost</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Design.</strong> Two rails or three, picket style, and top style all change the price.</li>
<li><strong>Height.</strong> Taller panels cost more.</li>
<li><strong>Gates.</strong> Pool gates need self-closing hinges and latches.</li>
<li><strong>Length.</strong> Long runs add up, which is why acreage usually switches to ranch fencing.</li>
<li><strong>Ground.</strong> Slopes toward the lake need racked or stepped panels.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>

<h2>Aluminum fence questions</h2>
<div class="faq76">
<details><summary>Aluminum or steel?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Aluminum doesn't rust, which makes it the usual pick near the water and around pools. Steel is heavier and much more rigid, so it's the stronger choice for drive gates and security. Many owners use both. See <a href="/resources/aluminum-vs-steel-fence/">aluminum vs. steel</a>.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Will an aluminum fence keep my dog in?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>It depends on the dog. Small dogs can slip between standard pickets, so ask about closer picket spacing or a mesh liner along the bottom.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Can I paint it a different color later?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>It can be done, but a field coat never holds up as well as the factory powder coat. Choose a color you and your HOA can live with, usually black or bronze, at the start.</p></div></details>
</div>
