<p><strong>Look.</strong> Ornamental metal fencing &mdash; often called wrought iron, even though today it's usually steel or aluminum &mdash; has slim vertical pickets, clean rails, and options like spear tops, finials, and decorative rings. It defines a yard and adds security without blocking light or views, which is why it's the go-to fence on golf-course and lakefront lots and around pools. Steel is the heavier of the two metals, with pickets and rails that resist bending.</p>

<p><strong>Upkeep.</strong> Powder-coated steel is heavier and stronger than aluminum but needs any scratches touched up so rust doesn't get a start. Clean the spot, let it dry, and cover it with a matching metal paint. Caught early, it takes a few minutes. Gates benefit from a yearly check of hinges and latches.</p>

<p><strong>Cost.</strong> On our <a href="/pricing-guide/">pricing guide</a>, steel typically runs $40&ndash;$100 per linear foot installed. Heavier commercial-grade panels, custom heights, and decorative gates add to the price. Looking for the rust-free option? See <a href="/materials/aluminum/">aluminum fencing</a>.</p>

<section class="section76-alt full-bleed76">
<div class="inner76">
<h2>Where steel earns the extra weight</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Drive gates.</strong> A wide gate at an acreage entrance hangs a lot of weight from one post. With an automatic opener, the frame has to stay rigid through thousands of cycles, and steel holds its shape far better than aluminum at that size.</li>
<li><strong>Frontage and security.</strong> Along a road or parking area where a vehicle might bump it, and on front-property fencing where security matters. See <a href="/resources/security-fence-types/">security fence types</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Big dogs.</strong> Steel holds up to a large dog jumping against it.</li>
<li><strong>Wooded lots.</strong> It holds up to a falling limb or a bump from a mower or truck far better than aluminum.</li>
<li><strong>Mixed runs.</strong> Many owners use aluminum around the pool and yard, and steel for the drive gate.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>

<h2>Acreage or subdivision lot?</h2>
<div class="cards76">
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On acreage</h3><p>On acreage, ornamental metal is usually used where it's seen most: entry and drive gates, the front of the property, and the yard or pool area close to the house. Running it along an entire multi-acre boundary is rarely cost-effective; ranch fencing usually takes over from there.</p></div></div>
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On subdivision lots</h3><p>On subdivision lots, ornamental metal fits front yards, side yards, and pool enclosures, and it's the most common choice on golf-course and lakefront lots where guidelines call for open fencing. If your property has HOA or deed restrictions, check with your board on height and style.</p></div></div>
</div>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section76">
<div class="prose76">
<h2>Local rules</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>City of Magnolia.</strong> Inside city limits, a fence needs a city permit: $65 plus a $50 administrative fee on the November 2024 schedule. The application asks for length, height, and material.</li>
<li><strong>City of Montgomery.</strong> Fences are on the city's list of projects that need a permit.</li>
<li><strong>Unincorporated Montgomery County.</strong> A typical residential fence usually doesn't need a county permit, but HOA or POA approval may still apply.</li>
<li><strong>Lake and golf communities.</strong> Bentwater, Walden, April Sound, and Grand Harbor have architectural committees that review fence plans, and open metal toward the water or fairway is the usual approved choice.</li>
<li><strong>Lake Conroe.</strong> On west-shore lots, the back of the lot can run into land the San Jacinto River Authority controls. We keep the fence off it.</li>
</ul>
<p>See <a href="/service-areas/magnolia-tx/">fencing in Magnolia</a> and our <a href="/faq/fence-permits-magnolia-montgomery/">permit guide</a>.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section76-alt full-bleed76">
<div class="inner76">
<h2>What affects the cost</h2>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Design.</strong> Two rails or three, picket style, and top style all change the price.</li>
<li><strong>Grade.</strong> Commercial-grade panels cost more than residential.</li>
<li><strong>Gates and openers.</strong> Drive gates and automatic openers are priced on their own.</li>
<li><strong>Height and length.</strong> Taller panels and long runs add up.</li>
<li><strong>Ground.</strong> Sloped lots need racked or stepped panels.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2>Ornamental steel questions</h2>
<div class="faq76">
<details><summary>Aluminum or steel?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Steel is heavier and much more rigid, so it's the stronger choice for drive gates, frontage, and security. Aluminum doesn't rust, which makes it the usual pick near the water and around pools. Many owners use both. See <a href="/resources/aluminum-vs-steel-fence/">aluminum vs. steel</a>.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Will a steel fence rust?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Not where the powder coat is intact. Rust starts where the coating chips down to bare metal. Touch up chips with a matching metal paint as soon as you see them.</p></div></details>
<details><summary>Can I paint it a different color later?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>It can be done, but a field coat never holds up as well as the factory powder coat. Choose a color you and your HOA can live with, usually black or bronze, at the start.</p></div></details>
</div>
