<p><strong>Look.</strong> Ornamental metal fencing &mdash; often called wrought iron, even though today it's usually steel or aluminum &mdash; has slim vertical pickets, clean rails, and options like spear tops, finials, and decorative rings. It defines a yard and adds security without blocking light or views, which is why it's the go-to fence on golf-course and lakefront lots and around pools.</p>

<p><strong>Upkeep.</strong> Aluminum doesn't rust, which makes it a strong pick near the water and around pool chemicals. Powder-coated steel is heavier and stronger but needs any scratches touched up so rust doesn't get a start. Either way, maintenance is mostly an occasional rinse. Gates benefit from a yearly check of hinges and latches, especially self-closing pool gates.</p>

<p><strong>Cost.</strong> Ornamental metal sits at the higher end of the cost range. Aluminum is typically a bit less than steel for similar styles, while heavier commercial-grade panels, custom heights, and decorative gates add to the price. On view lots and pool enclosures, many homeowners consider it worth the investment because nothing else does the job as well.</p>

<h2>How ornamental steel and aluminum holds up in the Magnolia area</h2>
<p class="section-intro76">Ornamental metal is the go-to fence where a view matters, and it handles our heat and humidity well when you pick the right metal for the spot.</p>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Humidity and rust.</strong> Aluminum doesn't rust, which makes it the better choice near the water, around pools, and in damp, shady yards. Powder-coated steel is stronger but needs scratches touched up so rust can't get started.</li>
<li><strong>Sun and heat.</strong> Metal won't warp, check, or fade the way wood can. A quality powder coat holds its color through long summers.</li>
<li><strong>Sandy and clay soils.</strong> Posts are set solidly so panels stay level and gates latch cleanly through wet and dry seasons. We request Texas 811 locates before any digging.</li>
<li><strong>Storm wind.</strong> The open design lets wind pass straight through, so ornamental fencing tends to come through storms better than solid privacy fences.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section76">
<h2>Acreage or subdivision lot?</h2>
<p class="section-intro76">Properties around Magnolia and Pinehurst range from tight subdivision lots to multi-acre tracts in unincorporated Montgomery County. Here's where ornamental steel and aluminum fits best.</p>
<div class="cards76">
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On acreage</h3><p>On acreage, ornamental metal is usually used where it's seen most: entry and drive gates, the front of the property, and the yard or pool area close to the house. Running it along an entire multi-acre boundary is rarely cost-effective; ranch fencing usually takes over from there.</p></div></div>
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On subdivision lots</h3><p>On subdivision lots, ornamental metal fits front yards, side yards, and pool enclosures, and it's the most common choice on golf-course and lakefront lots where guidelines call for open fencing. If your property has HOA or deed restrictions, check with your board on height and style.</p></div></div>
</div>
</section>
