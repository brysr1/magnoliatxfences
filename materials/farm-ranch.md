<p><strong>Look.</strong> Farm and ranch fencing covers several materials that each do a different job. Woven field wire and no-climb mesh are practical and nearly disappear from a distance. Barbed wire is the traditional cattle fence. Wood rail and board fencing gives road frontage and front pastures a classic ranch look, and welded pipe fencing is strong, clean, and long-lasting for pens, arenas, and entries.</p>

<p><strong>Upkeep.</strong> The longevity of any wire fence comes down to the corners and braces &mdash; built right, they keep the wire tight for years. After that, upkeep means walking the line after storms, clearing fallen limbs, and re-tensioning or splicing wire as needed. Wood rail benefits from occasional sealing, and painted pipe fencing may need touch-ups to keep rust away.</p>

<p><strong>Cost.</strong> Barbed and field wire are the most economical per foot, which matters when you're fencing hundreds or thousands of feet. No-climb horse fence costs more than standard field wire. Rail and pipe fencing are the premium end, so many owners use them where they're most visible and switch to wire across the back of the property.</p>
