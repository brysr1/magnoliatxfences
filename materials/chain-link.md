<p><strong>Look.</strong> Chain link is about function first. Standard galvanized silver is the most economical, while black or green vinyl-coated chain link blends into landscaping and looks noticeably more finished. It works well for dog runs, side yards, back property lines, sports areas, and commercial lots where visibility and security matter more than privacy. Privacy slats can be added if you need some screening.</p>

<p><strong>Upkeep.</strong> Very little. Galvanized and vinyl-coated wire both resist rust, and there's nothing to stain or seal. The main things to watch are gates and tension: a sagging gate or a loose stretch of fabric is usually a quick adjustment. Keeping vines off the fabric prevents it from pulling out of shape over time.</p>

<p><strong>Cost.</strong> Chain link is the most budget-friendly fence we install for most applications, especially on long runs. Vinyl coating, taller heights, heavier gauge wire, and commercial-grade posts raise the price, but it typically remains well below wood, vinyl, or ornamental metal per foot.</p>

<h2>How chain link holds up in the Magnolia area</h2>
<p class="section-intro76">Chain link is the practical workhorse of fencing, and it holds up well in our climate when it's built with the right coating and solid posts.</p>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Humidity and rust.</strong> Galvanized wire resists rust in our humid air, and black or green vinyl-coated chain link adds another layer of protection along with a more finished look.</li>
<li><strong>Sun and heat.</strong> There's nothing to stain, seal, or warp. Vinyl coatings are made to hold up to strong sun.</li>
<li><strong>Sandy and clay soils.</strong> Terminal and gate posts carry the tension of the whole fence, so we set them firmly for the soil on your lot. We request Texas 811 locates before any digging.</li>
<li><strong>Storm wind.</strong> Wind passes right through chain link. Falling limbs can bend a top rail, but those repairs are usually quick and inexpensive.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2>Acreage or subdivision lot?</h2>
<p class="section-intro76">Properties around Magnolia and Pinehurst range from tight subdivision lots to multi-acre tracts in unincorporated Montgomery County. Here's where chain link fits best.</p>
<div class="cards76">
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On acreage</h3><p>On acreage, chain link is an economical choice for dog runs, garden enclosures, equipment areas, and back property lines. For livestock, woven field fence or no-climb horse fence is usually the better fit, and we'll point you there when it makes more sense.</p></div></div>
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On subdivision lots</h3><p>On subdivision lots, chain link works well for side yards, dog runs, and back property lines. Some deed-restricted neighborhoods limit chain link where it's visible from the street, so if your property has HOA or deed restrictions, check with your board first.</p></div></div>
