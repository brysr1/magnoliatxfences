<p><strong>Look.</strong> Composite fencing is made from a blend of wood fibers and plastic, formed into boards with a wood-grain texture. Brand-name systems such as Trex are the best known, but several manufacturers make them. Composite comes in earthy browns and modern grays, and its solid boards make it a natural fit for full-privacy and horizontal-style fences that look high-end from day one.</p>

<p><strong>Upkeep.</strong> Composite doesn't need stain or sealer, won't rot, and resists the warping, splitting, and insect damage that wear down wood in a humid climate. An occasional wash keeps it looking fresh. Colors can lighten slightly in strong sun over the first months before settling in.</p>

<p><strong>Cost.</strong> Composite is one of the premium options &mdash; generally at the top of the range along with ornamental metal. The higher up-front cost buys you a fence that looks like wood without the ongoing maintenance, which appeals to homeowners planning to stay put for many years.</p>

<h2>How composite holds up in the Magnolia area</h2>
<p class="section-intro76">Composite was made for climates like ours: it gives you the look of wood without the rot, insects, and resealing that humidity brings.</p>
<ul class="checklist76">
<li><strong>Humidity and rain.</strong> Composite boards won't rot, warp, or split from moisture, and insects ignore them. An occasional wash takes care of mildew in shady spots.</li>
<li><strong>Sun and heat.</strong> Color can lighten slightly in the first months of strong sun before it settles. Dark boards can get warm to the touch in midsummer.</li>
<li><strong>Sandy and clay soils.</strong> Composite boards are heavier than wood, so posts and framing need to be solid and set for the soil on your lot. We request Texas 811 locates before any digging.</li>
<li><strong>Storm wind.</strong> Like any solid privacy fence, composite catches wind, so post setting and spacing matter as much as the boards themselves.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section76">
<h2>Acreage or subdivision lot?</h2>
<p class="section-intro76">Properties around Magnolia and Pinehurst range from tight subdivision lots to multi-acre tracts in unincorporated Montgomery County. Here's where composite fits best.</p>
<div class="cards76">
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On acreage</h3><p>On acreage, composite is best used where it's seen and enjoyed &mdash; privacy around the backyard, pool, or outdoor living area. It's a premium material, so it's rarely used for long boundary runs.</p></div></div>
<div class="card76"><div class="card76-body"><h3>On subdivision lots</h3><p>On subdivision lots, composite makes a high-end, low-maintenance privacy fence, including horizontal designs. Because it's a newer material, some HOAs haven't addressed it yet, so if your property has HOA or deed restrictions, check with your board before choosing it.</p></div></div>
</div>
</section>
