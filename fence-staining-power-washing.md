<h2>Why our weather is hard on wood</h2>
<p>Strong sun breaks down wood fibers and fades color. Humidity and heavy rain drive moisture in and out of the boards over and over, which leads to graying, splitting, and cupping, and the damp encourages mildew and algae. A quality stain or paint with UV blockers and a water repellent slows all of that down. Left unfinished, cedar and pine gray out and get more prone to splintering within a few years here.</p>
</div>
</section>

<h2>How the job goes</h2>
<ol class="steps76 steps76-long">
<li><h3>Wash and prep</h3><p>Power washing removes dirt, mildew, and failing old finish. We wire-brush and lightly sand rough or splintered spots.</p></li>
<li><h3>Let it dry</h3><p>Wood has to dry fully before stain or paint goes on, which can take longer during humid stretches.</p></li>
<li><h3>Stain, paint, or seal</h3><p>We apply the finish you chose for the look and coverage you want.</p></li>
</ol>

<h2>Staining and power washing questions</h2>
<div class="faq76">
<details><summary>How often should I refinish my wood fence?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>It depends on sun, rain, and the last product used. <details><summary>Should I stain or paint?</summary><div class="answer76"><p>Most people who want full coverage choose a solid stain over paint, because paint tends to peel or crack sooner outdoors. Semi-transparent stain is the pick if you want the grain to show.</p></div></details>
